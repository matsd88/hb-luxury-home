Huntington Beach Parking: Pier, Main Street and Beaches

By Mats, SuperhostUpdated October 22, 2026

Short answer: For a few hours at the Pier and on Main Street, park in the Main Promenade Parking Structure, 200 Main Street. The first 90 minutes are free. After that it is $1 per 20 minutes, capped at $15 a day from fall to spring. For a full beach day, the City's South Beach attended lots between First Street and Beach Boulevard are a flat $15 a day. Bolsa Chica and Huntington State Beach are also $15 and take cards only. The Pier Plaza lot right beside the Pier is $3 an hour, so it adds up past five hours. All of November 2026 is off-season, so these are the rates you will see this fall and winter.

With a disabled person placard, the hourly beach lots (Pier Plaza, Blufftop, First & Atlanta and Warner) are free in marked spaces. State Parks charges $7.50 with its own Disabled Discount Pass, and seniors 62+ pay $14.

The cheapest choice depends on how long you stay

  • Under 90 minutes: the Main Promenade structure is free. It is the obvious pick for coffee, a walk to the end of the Pier, or a quick look at the surf.
  • Two to five hours: stay in the structure. Off-season, three hours costs about $5 after the free 90 minutes. Pier Plaza would be $9. That covers a morning of fishing off the Pier, which needs no license.
  • All day on the sand: a South Beach attended lot or a state beach, at $15 flat. Off-season, the structure also caps at $15, so if you want Main Street for dinner afterward, it is a fair choice too.

The structure's parking day ends at 3 a.m., and parking for more than one day is charged when you exit. The City's beach parking page lists 830 spaces there.

Tuesdays: move your car before 2 p.m.

Surf City Nights closes Main Street between Pacific Coast Highway and Orange Avenue every Tuesday, 4:30 to 8:30 p.m., for a farmers market, crafters and live music. Downtown Huntington Beach asks drivers to move cars off downtown streets before 2 p.m. on Tuesdays so they are not towed. Park in the Main Promenade structure instead. The 90 free minutes cover a lap of the market.

Hours and curfews

  • City beach: the curfew is 10 p.m. to 5 a.m. and the City says it is strictly enforced. Off-street lots generally run 5 a.m. to midnight. Meters run 6 a.m. to midnight every day, holidays included, so there is no free Sunday.
  • State beaches: Bolsa Chica and Huntington State Beach are open 6 a.m. to 10 p.m. Entry gates close at 9 p.m., and the gate closes earlier if the lot fills.
  • Strand structure, 155 Fifth Street (enter on Sixth): open 9 a.m. to midnight. It is run separately, its rates are not on the City's rate page, and the City annual pass is not valid there. Read the sign at the entrance.

Thanksgiving weekend

Thanksgiving is Thursday, November 26, 2026. The City lists a $20 daily maximum for "holidays and special events" at the Main Promenade structure, but its beach parking page says those holiday rates run from May 1 through Labor Day. Expect the regular off-season prices on Thanksgiving weekend, and read the posted sign in case an event changes them. If a sunny holiday afternoon fills the downtown lots, Bolsa Chica is a good fallback. For plans that week, see the Thanksgiving week 2026 guide.

Is the annual pass worth it?

For most visitors, no. The City's annual beach parking permit is $195, or $100 for seniors 62+ and placard holders, and $125 for veterans. It covers the Main Promenade structure, the City beach lots and the metered spaces on PCH and Beach Boulevard. It does not cover the state beaches or the Strand structure. At $15 a day, it pays off after 13 beach days, so it only makes sense on a month-long stay with a daily beach habit. State Parks sells its own annual passes for Bolsa Chica and Huntington State Beach.

What we cannot tell you

The City's page has no effective date and says rates are subject to change. Pacific City, the shopping center across PCH, offers parking validation for shoppers, but it does not publish how many hours, so ask at the store. And we have no reliable source for free street parking near the Pier, so we have not listed any. Signs at the lot win over this guide. Big events change the rules too: on Surf City Marathon weekend, February 5–7, 2027, parts of PCH close and the race runs its own free shuttle lots, covered in the Surf City Marathon parking and stay guide.

Parking FAQ

Is there free parking near the Huntington Beach Pier?

Yes, for a short visit. The first 90 minutes are free at the Main Promenade Parking Structure, 200 Main Street, one block from the Pier. After that it is $1 per 20 minutes off-season, up to $15 a day.

How much is parking at Huntington Beach?

Off-season (after Labor Day weekend until Memorial Day weekend), the City charges $3 an hour at Pier Plaza and the other hourly beach lots and meters, and $15 a day at the South Beach attended lots. In summer it is $4 an hour and $20 a day.

How much is parking at Bolsa Chica and Huntington State Beach?

$15 per car per day most of the year, and $20 on summer weekends and holidays from Memorial Day weekend through Labor Day weekend. Both parks are cashless. Gates close at 9 p.m. and the parks close at 10 p.m.

Where do I park for Surf City Nights?

Use the Main Promenade structure at 200 Main Street, entering on Olive or Walnut. The first 90 minutes are free. Downtown asks drivers to move cars off downtown streets before 2 p.m. on Tuesdays so cars are not towed.

Is the Huntington Beach annual parking pass worth it?

For most visitors, no. The general pass is $195 a year, the price of 13 off-season days in a $15 lot. It can pay off for a long stay of daily beach trips, and seniors 62+ pay $100.
