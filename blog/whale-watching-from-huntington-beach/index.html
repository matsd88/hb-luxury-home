Whale Watching from Huntington Beach: A Winter 2026–27 Family Guide

By Mats, SuperhostUpdated October 18, 2026

Short answer: Gray whales pass the Orange County coast every winter. Newport Landing calls December through April the prime gray whale period, and NOAA says the northbound trip, with new calves, runs from mid-February to May. From Huntington Beach, the boats leave from three harbors: Newport Beach (from $33 for kids and $41 for adults on weekdays), Long Beach (next to the Aquarium of the Pacific) and Dana Point, which promises a free return ticket if you see no whale and few dolphins. Trips take 2 to 2.5 hours. Pick a morning boat, bring jackets, and if you can, plan around the Dana Point Festival of Whales on March 5–7, 2027.

This guide is for a family or group planning a winter trip to Huntington Beach who want to see a whale: when to come, which boat to book, what it costs, and what to do if the sea is too rough.

When to go: the gray whale calendar

Eastern North Pacific gray whales make one of the longest migrations of any mammal, about 10,000 miles round trip, according to NOAA Fisheries. They head south in the fall to the lagoons of Mexico, where calves are born from early January to mid-February. From mid-February to May they swim back north along the West Coast.

  • December and January: whales heading south. Newport Landing says December through April is the prime gray whale period.
  • Mid-February to May: whales heading north, some with calves (NOAA).
  • Dana Point's window is wider: the Dana Wharf marine life calendar lists gray whales from November to May.

Christmas break and Presidents' Day week both fall in the season, so a school-holiday trip can include a whale boat. Sightings are never certain, so do not save your only try for the last day.

Which harbor? Newport, Long Beach or Dana Point

Newport Beach: the most departures

Newport Landing runs trips every day of the year. From November to February its standard boats leave at 9:00 a.m., 12:00 p.m. and 2:30 p.m. on weekdays and weekends. Trips last 2 to 2.5 hours.

  • Adults (13–59): $41 weekdays, $45 weekends and holidays
  • Juniors (3–12) and seniors (60+): $33 weekdays, $37 weekends and holidays
  • Toddlers (0–2): $8

A family of two adults and two kids pays $148 on a weekday. Its FAQ asks you to arrive 60 minutes before departure. Check-in is at 309 Palm Street or 400 Main Street on the Balboa Peninsula, a short walk apart, and you will not know which one until your trip is assigned. Strollers stay on the dock, and standard boats have restrooms. Sightings are not guaranteed. If no whales are seen, the FAQ offers a discounted return ticket. Nearby city lots and meters are paid by app.

Long Beach: whales and the aquarium in one day

Harbor Breeze Cruises leaves from 100 Aquarium Way, Dock #2, right next to the Aquarium of the Pacific. Its whale and dolphin trip runs 2.5 hours, with morning and afternoon departures. Walk-up prices are $45 for adults (12+) on weekdays and $50 on weekends, $40 for seniors (62+), $30 for children 3 to 11, and free under 3. Adult and senior tickets cost less online. The aquarium is about 25 minutes from the house, so this makes an easy full day: boat in the morning, aquarium after lunch.

Dana Point: the sighting promise

Dana Wharf Whale Watching, at 34675 Golden Lantern, runs 2-hour whale and dolphin trips. Its promise: you will see a whale or a good showing of dolphins, as the captain decides. If not, you get a "Whale Check" for a free return trip that never expires. Check its booking page for current prices and times. Dana Point is the longest drive of the three from Huntington Beach, so it suits a full day out.

Those same three harbors are where the Catalina ferries dock. For crossing times, winter schedules and an Avalon day, see the Catalina Island day-trip guide.

The Festival of Whales: March 5–7, 2027

Dana Point's Festival of Whales holds its 56th year on March 5 to 7, 2027, during the northbound migration. Visit Dana Point says it fills the harbor with a parade, family activities, marine science and whale-watching cruises. Most events are free, but cruises are ticketed separately. Final times and the parade schedule had not been posted as of mid-October 2026. For a March trip, book the house now and the boat when tickets open.

Watching from land, for free

If someone in the group gets seasick, watch from a cliff instead. The American Cetacean Society's gray whale census counts whales from the Point Vicente Interpretive Center in Rancho Palos Verdes, every day in daylight from December 1 to late May, from a post 138 feet above the water. It is on the Palos Verdes Peninsula, past Long Beach, and it makes a good half-day drive with a picnic.

Tips for a family whale boat

  • Go in the morning. Newport Landing's FAQ suggests a morning trip if you get motion sickness. It also says to take any seasickness medicine about an hour before you leave, look at the horizon, drink water and eat a light meal.
  • Dress warm. It is cooler on the water. Bring a jacket or sweatshirt, sunglasses and sunscreen, and wear flat rubber-soled shoes. Heels and hard soles are not allowed.
  • Book a weekday. Newport and Long Beach charge less Monday to Friday.
  • Keep a backup day. Newport Landing says departures can change without notice and a minimum number of passengers is needed for a trip to go.
  • Add the wetlands. Bolsa Chica says November to February is its best season for shorebirds, ducks and loons. It is about 10 minutes from the house. The reserve guide covers parking, trails and rules.

A 4-night winter whale weekend

  • Thursday: Check in at 4:00 p.m. Pizza and a pool tournament.
  • Friday: 9:00 a.m. Newport boat, weekday price. Afternoon on Balboa Island.
  • Saturday: Bolsa Chica in the morning, beach in the afternoon. The bonfire guide covers where the fire rings are.
  • Sunday: Long Beach: morning whale boat, then the Aquarium of the Pacific. If Friday had no whales, this is your second try.
  • Monday: Checkout is at 11:00 a.m.

Whale-watching FAQ

When is whale season in Huntington Beach?

Gray whales pass in winter and spring. Newport Landing calls December through April the prime period, Dana Wharf lists November to May, and NOAA says the northbound trip with calves runs from mid-February to May.

Can you go whale watching from Huntington Beach itself?

The boats this guide covers leave from Newport Beach, Long Beach (next to the Aquarium of the Pacific) and Dana Point. All three are an easy drive from a Huntington Beach base.

How much is a whale-watching trip?

Newport Landing charges $41 for adults and $33 for kids 3–12 on weekdays, and $45 and $37 on weekends. Harbor Breeze in Long Beach charges $45 for adults on weekdays, $50 on weekends, and $30 for kids 3–11 at the walk-up window.

Are whale sightings guaranteed?

No. Dana Wharf gives a free return ticket if you see neither a whale nor a good showing of dolphins. Newport Landing offers a discounted return ticket if no whales are seen.

When is the 2027 Festival of Whales?

March 5 to 7, 2027, in Dana Point Harbor. Most events are free. Whale-watching cruises are ticketed separately.
