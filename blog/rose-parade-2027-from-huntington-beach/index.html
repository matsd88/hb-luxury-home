Rose Parade 2027 from Huntington Beach: Seats, Timing and the Train

By Mats, SuperhostUpdated October 27, 2026

Short answer: yes, you can see the Rose Parade on a day trip from Huntington Beach. The 2027 parade is on Friday, January 1, 2027 at 8 a.m. in Pasadena, about 36 miles from our house. That is roughly an hour by car with no traffic. With a grandstand seat on Orange Grove Boulevard, where you have to be seated by 6:30 a.m., leave Huntington Beach by about 4:30 a.m. Seats cost $82 to $133 each, fees included. Watching from the sidewalk is free, but the best curb spots are claimed from noon on December 31 by people who camp overnight, so a day-tripper usually buys a seat. There is also a car-light option: park at a Metro station in Long Beach and ride the A Line train straight into Pasadena.

Dates, times and rules below come from the Tournament of Roses, its parade day guide and the official ticket seller, Sharp Seating, checked October 27, 2026. This guide is for someone spending the holidays in Orange County who wants the parade as one big day of the trip. If you are only coming for the parade, read the honest section on where to stay first.

Option 1: Drive, with a grandstand seat

This is the simplest plan for a group. Buy seats from Sharp Seating, add a reserved parking pass, and drive up before dawn.

  • Seat prices (October 27, 2026): Area 1, near the start on Orange Grove and West Colorado, is $120 to $133. The $120 seats are on South Orange Grove in the early formation area, which Sharp Seating discounts. Area 2 is $103. Areas 3 and 4, farther east on Colorado, are $82 to $103, and Area 5 is $82. Seats are reserved and numbered. Tickets are digital and go out by December 1, 2026.
  • When to be seated: the Tournament asks ticket holders on Orange Grove (inside the security zone) to arrive by 6:30 a.m., seats west of Fair Oaks Avenue by 7 a.m., and everyone else by 8 a.m. It also says to plan on arriving at least two hours early.
  • Leaving Huntington Beach: it is about 36 miles from our house in 92649 to the start of the route, roughly an hour with no traffic. At 4 a.m. on New Year's Day the freeways are quiet; the slow part is the last few miles, around the closures. Leave by about 4:30 a.m. for Orange Grove seats, or 5 to 5:30 a.m. for seats farther east.
  • Parking: Sharp Seating's 2027 brochure lists reserved car parking at $57.50 to $67.50. If you buy a pass, Sharp Seating says not to use GPS. Follow the directions on its parking page instead, because GPS will route you into closed streets. Free street parking exists, but anything on the route will be impounded, and red curbs and No Parking signs are enforced.
  • Bags: there is a clear-bag-only policy for the parade and all its ticketed events. In its 2026 coverage, NBC Los Angeles also reported that umbrellas were not allowed, so pack a rain layer instead.

Option 2: Park in Long Beach and take the Metro A Line

This is the local trick most out-of-town guides miss. Metro's A Line light rail runs from Downtown Long Beach through Los Angeles to Pasadena in one ride, with no transfer. From Huntington Beach, drive about 16 miles (around 25 minutes) to Willow Street station in Long Beach, which has a park-and-ride lot with about 1,000 spaces. Then ride north to Memorial Park, Lake or Allen station in Pasadena, each a short walk from Colorado Boulevard.

  • Fare: $1.75 a ride, and Metro caps it at $5 a day. Buy a TAP card or use the TAP app.
  • Time: Long Beach to Pasadena is a long ride, well over an hour. Check Metro's trip planner for New Year's morning before you commit, and leave earlier than you think.
  • Service: the Tournament's parade day guide says Metro Rail runs 24 hours from the night of December 31 into parade morning, with extra service on parade day. Confirm on metro.net in late December, because holiday schedules get posted close to the date.
  • Why bother: no Pasadena parking to find, no closures to drive around, and nobody has to drive home tired.

Option 3: Watch free from the curb

Most of the route is free to watch from the sidewalk, and plenty of families camp overnight. According to the Tournament, camping along the route starts at noon on December 31. At 11 p.m. you can move out to the blue Honor Line painted on the street, but not past it. Overnight street parking in Pasadena also opens at noon the day before. The City of Pasadena's rules for the 2026 parade, reported by NBC Los Angeles, banned tents, sofas and boxes used as seats, bonfires, open alcohol, drones, and throwing anything into the route, even marshmallows. Small barbecues raised at least a foot off the ground and 25 feet from buildings were allowed. Anyone under 18 needed an adult on the route overnight. The city posts its 2027 notice in December, so check it then.

From Huntington Beach, the honest version is this: arriving at dawn without a seat usually means standing several rows back. If the curb is the experience you want, send one or two people up on the afternoon of December 31 to hold the space, and have the rest come on the train in the morning.

See the floats up close: Floatfest and the other events

On TV and from the grandstands the floats look painted. Up close you can see that the surfaces are flowers, seeds, leaves and bark. Seeing them parked is also much easier to fit around a beach trip than the parade itself.

  • Floatfest (floats parked after the parade): Friday, January 1, 2027, 1:30 to 5 p.m.; Saturday, January 2, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m., with 7 to 9 a.m. reserved for seniors and visitors with disabilities; and Sunday, January 3, 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. Last entry is an hour before closing. It is at Sierra Madre Boulevard and Washington Boulevard in Pasadena, with free park-and-ride shuttles. The Tournament warns that the shuttles run slower than normal. Tickets were $26.50 last season.
  • Decorating Places (watch volunteers finish the floats in the barns): $21.50 last season, December 28–30.
  • Bandfest (parade bands in concert): $26.50 last season, December 29 and 30.
  • Equestfest (the parade's horses and riders, at the Los Angeles Equestrian Center in Burbank): $26.50 general admission last season, December 29.

Sharp Seating's brochure says those event prices and dates reflect last season and may change. Check its events page for the 2027 details. A relaxed plan for families: watch the parade on TV over breakfast at the house, then drive up for Floatfest on January 2.

The Rose Bowl Game on the same day

The 2027 Rose Bowl Game is a College Football Playoff quarterfinal on January 1, 2027, at Rose Bowl Stadium. The Tournament says kickoff will be at 1 p.m. or 5 p.m. Pacific, announced with the bracket on Sunday, December 6. Tickets started at $202.90 each, fees included, on Ticketmaster. Sharp Seating says its Area 1 grandstands are about 1.5 miles from the stadium, so parade-then-game on foot is realistic. Either way, plan on a long day before the drive back to the coast.

Rose Parade from Huntington Beach FAQ

When is the 2027 Rose Parade?

Friday, January 1, 2027, at 8 a.m. in Pasadena. It is the 138th parade, the theme is "Welcome," and it lasts about two hours along a 5.5-mile route from Green Street and Orange Grove Boulevard to Sierra Madre Boulevard and Villa Street.

How early should I leave Huntington Beach for the Rose Parade?

Pasadena is about 36 miles from Huntington Beach, roughly an hour with no traffic. Grandstand ticket holders on Orange Grove Boulevard must arrive by 6:30 a.m., so leave by about 4:30 a.m. For seats farther east on Colorado Boulevard, leave by 5 to 5:30 a.m.

How much are Rose Parade grandstand tickets for 2027?

$82 to $133 per seat, fees included, from Sharp Seating, the official ticket office (checked October 27, 2026). Seats near the start on Orange Grove and West Colorado cost $120 to $133; seats farther east on Colorado cost $82 to $103.

Can you watch the Rose Parade for free?

Yes, from the sidewalk. Camping along the route starts at noon on December 31, and at 11 p.m. you can move up to the blue Honor Line on the street. For the 2026 parade the city banned tents, sofas, bonfires, open alcohol, drones and throwing objects into the route.

Can you take the train from Huntington Beach to the Rose Parade?

Not directly, but close. Drive about 16 miles to Willow Street station in Long Beach, park, and ride the Metro A Line north to Memorial Park, Lake or Allen station in Pasadena without changing trains. The fare is $1.75, capped at $5 a day. Allow well over an hour for the ride.
