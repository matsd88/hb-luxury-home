King Tides in Huntington Beach: Thanksgiving and Christmas Eve Times, and Where PCH Floods

By Mats, SuperhostUpdated November 11, 2026

Short answer: The next king tides in Huntington Beach are Tuesday to Thursday, November 24–26 (Thanksgiving week), Wednesday to Friday, December 23–25, and January 21–22, 2027. The water peaks in the morning, between about 7:30 and 9:30 a.m., at roughly 7 feet. The Christmas Eve high, about 7.2 feet at 8:26 a.m., is the highest tide predicted for all of 2026 at the nearest NOAA station. The part that matters for a visitor is the road: during October's high tides the City closed Pacific Coast Highway between Warner Avenue and Anderson Street in Sunset Beach from 8 a.m. to noon. On a king tide morning, don't plan a drive north on PCH, and don't park in a low spot by the harbour.

This guide is for someone staying in Huntington Beach over Thanksgiving or Christmas who saw the October flooding on the news and wants to know two things: will it happen while I'm here, and can I go and see it? I pulled the tide predictions and recent water levels from NOAA on November 11, 2026, and read the California Coastal Commission's King Tides pages and the news reports from the October event. Where nobody knows yet, I say so.

Why the tide table may be too low this year

A tide table only accounts for the moon and the sun. It leaves out wind, waves and how warm and high the ocean is running. This winter that gap is large. The Coastal Commission's King Tides page warns that scientists expect a strong El Niño, and that "actual water heights" can exceed the predictions.

You can see it in NOAA's own numbers. On the morning of October 27, the Los Angeles tide gauge peaked at 8.17 feet, against a prediction of 6.80. That is 1.4 feet over the table. From November 8 to 11 it was still running about 1.2 feet above the prediction. Nobody can say whether that will last until Thanksgiving. But the Thanksgiving and Christmas highs are predicted a few inches higher than the October 27 high was. If the ocean is still running high, expect the October flooding again, or worse.

The forecast that counts is the one issued a day or two ahead. The National Weather Service office in San Diego covers the Orange County coast and posts coastal flood advisories and warnings at weather.gov/sgx.

What flooded in October

  • PCH in Sunset Beach. The City closed Pacific Coast Highway between Warner Avenue and Anderson Street from 8 a.m. to noon on Tuesday, October 27, around a morning high (ABC7, FOX 11). That is the main road from Huntington Beach north to Seal Beach and Long Beach.
  • Huntington Harbour. Water came over the edges of the channels into residential streets, and City crews pumped it off Bayview Drive (FOX 11).
  • Newport Beach. Water reached the streets around 9 a.m. on October 26, and the Balboa Fun Zone, on the bay side of the Balboa Peninsula, flooded despite sandbags (ABC7). Newport Beach declared local emergencies on October 9 and October 19 (FOX 11).
  • Long Beach Peninsula. Water came over the sand berms at Bayshore Beach and flooded a below-ground garage (ABC7).

The pattern is the same everywhere. The trouble comes from the harbour and bay side, where water rises up behind low seawalls, more than from the open beach. And it happens over a few hours around the morning high, not all day.

How to plan a king tide morning

  1. Drive inland if you're heading north. On the mornings in the table, plan as if PCH between Warner Avenue and Anderson Street could be closed from about two hours before high tide to two hours after. For Seal Beach, Long Beach or LAX, take the 405. Check Caltrans QuickMap for live closures, since PCH is a state highway.
  2. Never drive through water on the road. Seawater hides how deep it is and wrecks engines and electrics. FOX 11 passed on AAA's advice to turn around even when it looks shallow. That goes double for a rental car.
  3. Don't leave the car low overnight. If you're parking near the harbour, in Sunset Beach or on the Balboa Peninsula the night before, pick higher ground.
  4. Do your beach time in the afternoon. The same tides that push water up in the morning pull it far out in the afternoon. Every day in the table has a minus tide between about 3 and 5 p.m. That is the best tide-pooling of the winter. The tide pools guide covers Crystal Cove and Little Corona.
  5. Thanksgiving runners: the Turkey Wobble starts at PCH and Beach Boulevard with waves from 7:00 to 9:00 a.m., and that morning's high is about 9:18. The race site hasn't mentioned the tide. Check it the day before, and if you're coming from north of Warner Avenue, don't take PCH through Sunset Beach.

Where to watch it safely

The Coastal Commission runs the King Tides Project. It asks people to photograph the highest water, ideally within half an hour of the predicted peak, next to a landmark like a seawall, pier, stairway or road, and upload the photo. It is a good reason to be out early on Christmas Eve. Its safety tips are short: obey cones and closures, never turn your back on the ocean, stay off rocks and seawalls, and don't flush shorebirds that have been pushed higher up than usual.

  • Bolsa Chica Ecological Reserve (about 10 minutes from the house). At a king tide the high water covers the mudflats. The Bolsa Chica Conservancy's bird page suggests arriving about an hour after the daytime high, when the water starts to drop and hundreds of shorebirds come in to feed on the mud. So aim for about 9:30 a.m. on December 24. The Bolsa Chica guide has the lots and trails.
  • The Huntington Beach Pier. From the deck you can see how far up the sand the water reaches. Stay off the beach below it if the surf is up, and follow lifeguard closures.
  • Huntington Harbour and Sunset Beach. This is where the flooding is, as in the photo above. These are people's homes, so watch from a public sidewalk, keep off flooded streets and out of the way of City crews.
  • The Balboa Peninsula in Newport Beach, where the bay side around the Fun Zone flooded in October. Same rules: don't drive into water, and expect cones and closures.

I'd skip the jetties at the Santa Ana River mouth and at the Newport Harbor entrance on these mornings. Rocks and seawalls are exactly where the Coastal Commission tells people not to stand.

What I couldn't find

  • I found no City of Huntington Beach closure plan for the November or December tides yet. In October, the PCH closure window was announced ahead of the morning high (FOX 11), so check the news and QuickMap the evening before.
  • No king tide events in Orange County are on the Coastal Commission's events list so far. The ones listed are in San Francisco and San Diego.
  • NOAA doesn't publish a tide table for the open beach at the Huntington Beach Pier itself. The Newport Bay Entrance and Los Angeles stations bracket it, and their predictions are within about 10 minutes and a couple of inches of each other on these days.

King tides FAQ

When are the king tides in Huntington Beach in 2026?

The California Coastal Commission's King Tides dates for Southern California are November 24–26 and December 23–25, 2026, and January 21–22, 2027. The highest water comes in the morning: about 7:50 to 9:20 a.m. at Thanksgiving, 7:40 to 9:15 a.m. at Christmas, and 7:35 to 8:25 a.m. in January, per NOAA's Newport Bay Entrance predictions.

Does PCH flood in Huntington Beach during king tides?

It can. During the late-October 2026 high tides, the City closed Pacific Coast Highway between Warner Avenue and Anderson Street in Sunset Beach from 8 a.m. to noon, and crews pumped water off Bayview Drive in Huntington Harbour. On a king tide morning, plan as if that stretch could be closed for about two hours either side of high tide, and use the 405 or inland streets instead.

Will the king tides be higher than the tide tables say?

Possibly. Tide tables only cover the moon and sun. In late October 2026 the Los Angeles tide gauge peaked at 8.17 feet against a 6.80-foot prediction, and from November 8 to 11 it was still running about 1.2 feet above the table. The Coastal Commission warns that this winter's El Niño can push water above predictions.

Is the Thanksgiving Turkey Wobble affected by the king tide?

The race site hasn't said so. The Turkey Wobble on November 26 runs on the beach path from PCH and Beach Boulevard with waves from 7:00 to 9:00 a.m., and that morning's high tide is about 9:18 a.m. Check the race site the day before, and if you're driving in from north of Warner Avenue, avoid PCH through Sunset Beach.
