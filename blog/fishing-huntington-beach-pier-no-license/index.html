Fishing the Huntington Beach Pier: No License, the Rules, and What You Can Eat

By Mats, SuperhostUpdated October 29, 2026

Short answer: You do not need a fishing license to fish from the Huntington Beach Pier, at any age. The California Department of Fish and Wildlife (CDFW) lists it as a free public fishing pier. What you still have to follow: the state's size and bag limits, a Spiny Lobster Report Card ($12.70) if you hoop-net for lobster, and the City's pier rules: no overhead casting over the top rail, two lines per person, and nobody on the pier from midnight to 5 a.m. Step off the pier onto the sand and the exemption ends: anyone 16 or older fishing from the beach needs a license.

This guide is for a visitor who has a free morning, a kid who wants to catch something, and a rod in the car or the idea of buying a cheap one. It comes from CDFW's free fishing piers page, CDFW's license prices, CDFW's lobster page, the City's beach rules in Huntington Beach Municipal Code chapter 13.08, and the state's fish advisory for this stretch of coast, all checked October 29, 2026.

Why no license: what counts as a public pier

California's license exemption is written into state regulations, not left to each city. CDFW says a qualifying pier has to be connected to the shoreline, offer free and unrestricted public access, and have been built, or now be used, mainly for fishing. The Huntington Beach Pier is on CDFW's Orange County list. Everyone on it can fish without a license, residents and visitors, adults and children.

Two things trip people up:

  • The exemption is the pier, not the area around it. Cast from the sand on either side and you need a license if you are 16 or older. That includes the surf fishing you will see at dawn south of the pier.
  • Everything else still applies. CDFW says size limits, bag limits, report cards and seasons apply on a pier just as they do anywhere. Its piers page also limits you to two rods or lines, and species-specific gear rules still apply.

Buying a license is quick if you want the beach too: CDFW sells them online, and the one-day license is $21.09 in 2026 whether or not you live in California.

The City's pier rules

Huntington Beach Municipal Code section 13.08.320, "Fishing From the Pier," starts with "Fishing is allowed from the Pier" and then lists what is not allowed. In plain terms:

  • No overhead casting. You may not cast a line over the top railing. Most people drop or swing a line out underhand, over the side. The pier is crowded with walkers, and a hook at head height is the reason for the rule.
  • Cast carefully. No casting a line, trap or mussel hook in a way that endangers anyone.
  • Two lines, two traps. No more than two lines in the water per person, and no more than two traps. Traps must be attended at all times.
  • Keep the walkway clear. A pole may not stick more than four feet in from the rail.
  • No cleaning on the benches, deck or rails. Cut or clean fish and bait only in areas provided for it. If you do not see one, bag the fish whole and clean it later.
  • Do not feed the birds. No food, bait or anything else that feeds wildlife on the pier. The pelicans and gulls will still watch your bait bucket.
  • Respect closed areas, and do not lever lines or traps against the railing.

Section 13.08.240 closes the pier and Pier Plaza from midnight to 5 a.m. unless the City gives you a permit. Evening fishing under the lights is fine; plan to be off by midnight. After the clocks go back on Sunday, November 1, sunset is around 5 p.m., so a November evening session is mostly in the dark. The November weather guide has sunset times.

Lobster season: hoop nets from the pier

California spiny lobster season is open now. CDFW's rule is that it opens at 6 p.m. on the Friday before the first Wednesday in October and closes at 11:59 p.m. on the first Wednesday after March 15. For this season that is Friday, October 2, 2026 to Wednesday, March 17, 2027.

  • You need a Spiny Lobster Report Card, even though you do not need a license on the pier. CDFW says this explicitly for public piers. The card is $12.70 for the season, and children under 16 need their own card too.
  • Two hoop nets per person from a public pier. The city's rule also requires you to attend them at all times.
  • Limit seven lobsters, each at least 3 1/4 inches measured along the carapace. You need a measuring device with you, and CDFW recommends a fixed-arm lobster gauge over a tape.
  • No hook-and-line lobster. A lobster caught on a fishing hook while you are fishing for fish must go straight back in the water.
  • Return the card or report online by April 30, 2027, even if you caught nothing.

Hoop-netting is a night activity for most people, which fits the pier's hours until midnight. Read CDFW's lobster page for how to fill in the card before you start.

Can you eat what you catch?

Mostly yes, with limits. The state's Office of Environmental Health Hazard Assessment (OEHHA) has a fish advisory for the coast south of Seal Beach Pier to San Mateo Point, which covers Huntington Beach. It is based on mercury and PCBs. The list reads like a list of what people catch off a Southern California pier:

A serving is about the size and thickness of your hand. OEHHA also says to eat only the skinless fillet and let the juices drain away when you cook, because these chemicals collect in skin and fat. Separately, CDFW posts health advisories for algal toxins, which can close shellfish or crab at times; check it before you keep anything.

For the fish you keep, the statewide general limit is 20 finfish in total, no more than 10 of one species, and many species have their own size limits and bag limits. California halibut, the prize fish here, must be at least 22 inches long, with a limit of five. CDFW's ocean sport fishing regulations list every species. If you cannot identify a fish, release it.

A simple plan for a pier morning

  1. Park in the Main Promenade structure at 200 Main Street; the first 90 minutes are free, and off-season it is $1 per 20 minutes after that, up to $15 a day.
  2. Bring everything. We have not confirmed a bait and tackle shop operating on the pier this fall, so buy bait and any tackle before you go. A small rolling cart helps on an 1,850-foot walk.
  3. Pack: a measuring tape or ruler (and a lobster gauge for hoop nets), pliers, a bag and a small cooler with ice for anything you keep, and a layer, because it is windier and cooler at the end of the pier than on the sand.
  4. Pick a spot along the rail without crowding anyone, and drop your line over the side rather than casting overhead.
  5. Take it home whole, on ice, and clean it there or at a designated area.

Kids usually do best with small hooks and a short session; the walk out, the pelicans and the surfers under the pier are half the fun. If they get bored, the Huntington Beach with kids guide has the rest of the day.

When the pier is the wrong choice

The pier is the easiest fishing in town, not the best fishing in the region. If you want a real chance at bigger fish, a half-day sportfishing boat out of Newport Beach or Long Beach is a different trip, and you need a California fishing license on a boat (ask whether the landing sells day licenses). The calm water of Huntington Harbour suits kayak fishing, also with a license. And Bolsa Chica's wetlands are an ecological reserve with their own rules; the Bolsa Chica guide covers where fishing is and is not allowed there.

What could change

CDFW license prices change each calendar year, so the 2026 prices above will rise on January 1, 2027. The City can close parts of the pier for events, repairs or high surf, and CDFW notes it cannot guarantee any pier is open. The OEHHA advisory dates from 2009, with posters updated in 2019 and 2023. Signs on the pier and CDFW's current rules win over this guide.

Huntington Beach Pier fishing FAQ

Do I need a fishing license to fish off the Huntington Beach Pier?

No. The California Department of Fish and Wildlife says no license is needed to fish recreationally from a public pier in ocean or bay waters, and it lists the Huntington Beach Pier as one of those piers in Orange County. Size limits, bag limits, seasons and report cards still apply.

Do I need a license to fish from the beach next to the pier?

Yes, if you are 16 or older. The pier exemption covers the pier only. On the sand you need a California sport fishing license. In 2026 a one-day license is $21.09 and a two-day license is $32.40, for residents and visitors alike.

Can I hoop-net for lobster from the Huntington Beach Pier?

Yes, in season, with a Spiny Lobster Report Card. The 2026-27 season runs from 6 p.m. on Friday, October 2, 2026, to 11:59 p.m. on Wednesday, March 17, 2027. The card costs $12.70, and children under 16 need one too. You can use up to two hoop nets from a public pier and must stay with them. The limit is seven lobsters, each at least 3 1/4 inches across the carapace.

What are the Huntington Beach Pier fishing rules?

City code allows fishing from the pier but bans overhead casting over the top rail, more than two lines or two traps per person, poles sticking more than four feet into the walkway, cleaning fish on benches, the deck or the railings, feeding wildlife, and fishing in areas closed to fishing. Nobody may be on the pier between midnight and 5 a.m.

Is it safe to eat fish caught off the Huntington Beach Pier?

Most species, in moderation. The state's fish advisory for the coast south of Seal Beach Pier to San Mateo Point says women 18 to 49 and children should not eat black croaker or Pacific barracuda, and should eat other species such as white croaker or halibut only once a week. Jacksmelt and Pacific chub mackerel are among the safest choices.
