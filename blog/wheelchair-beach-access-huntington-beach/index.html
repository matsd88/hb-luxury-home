Wheelchair Access at Huntington Beach: Beach Mats, Free Beach Wheelchairs and Placard Parking

By Mats, SuperhostUpdated November 8, 2026

Short answer: Yes, someone who uses a wheelchair can get onto the sand in Huntington Beach, and the help is free. Beach wheelchairs, with wide balloon wheels made for sand, are lent free at Huntington City Beach, Huntington State Beach and Bolsa Chica State Beach. City Beach also has two Mobi-Mats, firm roll-out walkways across the sand: one in front of the Waterfront Beach Resort by the Huntington Street entrance, and one north of the Pier. The two state beaches have wide paved ramps that run from the beach path out across the sand. Call the day before for a chair. City Beach may have only one.

This guide is for a family bringing someone who uses a wheelchair or a walker, or who just can't manage soft sand, to Huntington Beach for Thanksgiving or Christmas week, and who wants them down by the water instead of waiting in the car. I read the City's beach pages and 2026 parking rate sheet, the California State Parks accessibility pages for both state beaches, and Visit Huntington Beach's accessibility guide on November 8, 2026. Where the sources disagree or say nothing, I say so.

Free beach wheelchairs: where to ask

A beach wheelchair is a manual chair with big, wide wheels that roll over sand. Someone has to push it. You may be asked to leave an ID while you have it. Some places take reservations, and others lend first come, first served (Visit Huntington Beach).

The two Mobi-Mats on City Beach

A Mobi-Mat is a firm, roll-out walkway laid on the sand. A regular wheelchair, a walker, a cane or a stroller can use it without sinking. Huntington Beach laid its first one in May 2021 (ABC7). Visit Huntington Beach lists two on City Beach:

  • In front of the Waterfront Beach Resort, by the Huntington Street entrance to the big beach lot south of the Pier.
  • North of the Pier.

Visit Huntington Beach says the mats reach the water's edge at high tide. At low tide the water pulls back, so there will be a stretch of open sand past the end of the mat. If the goal is toes in the water without a beach wheelchair, go closer to high tide. NOAA's tide predictions for the Newport Bay Entrance are the nearest official table. If you're making a special trip, call (714) 536-8083 first to check the mat is in place.

Paved ramps at the two state beaches

The state beaches don't use mats. They have Spencer ramps, wide concrete paths that run from the beach bike path across the sand toward the high tide line. They stop short of the water.

  • Huntington State Beach: six ramps, next to Lots 1, 2, 4, 7, 9 and 10. South of Lot 1, two more wide paths run about 580 feet toward the ocean along the Talbert flood control channel. State Parks calls those "generally accessible."
  • Bolsa Chica State Beach: two ramps, one next to Lot 18 and one next to the campground.

All of them connect to the flat, paved beach path that runs about 8.5 miles from Bolsa Chica to the Santa Ana River. It is the easiest way to roll along the coast without touching sand at all. It is shared with bikes and e-bikes, so stay to the right. The bike path rules guide explains the 10 mph limit riders are supposed to follow.

Restrooms and picnic tables

State Parks is unusually specific about this, and it helps to know before you pick a lot.

  • Bolsa Chica State Beach: accessible restrooms in every day-use lot and along the trail. Accessible picnic areas are at the south end of Lot 19, the north end of Lot 17 and the south end of Lot 14, with accessible parking and paths. All of them can be reserved.
  • Huntington State Beach: the restrooms at the north end of Lot 1 were closed for accessibility upgrades as of January 2025. The ones at the south end of Lot 1 and at both ends of Lot 7 are usable, but State Parks lists barriers: narrow doorways, tight turning space, grab bars at uneven heights, and flush pedals and sink controls that take more than 5 pounds of force. Two covered picnic areas at Lot 1 and four at Lot 7 are accessible.
  • City Beach: Visit Huntington Beach says accessible restrooms are near the main beach entrances. Neither it nor the City names which ones.

The State Parks Disabled Discount Pass

The Disabled Discount Pass costs $3.50, lasts five years, and cuts day-use fees by half at more than 100 California state parks. It is for people with permanent disabilities. The application requirements take a government photo ID plus one proof, such as the DMV placard ID card or receipt in the disabled person's name, a vehicle registration showing disabled status, or a signed doctor's certification. State Parks says out-of-state placards are accepted. It doesn't say how long processing takes, so if your trip is at Thanksgiving, apply online now or just plan to pay $15 at the gate. Veterans should also look at the Distinguished Veteran Pass, which is free if you qualify.

A simple afternoon that works

  1. The day before: call the beach you picked and ask whether a beach wheelchair will be there. For City Beach, try both numbers in the table.
  2. Pick a beach by what you need. To stay on a mat in your own chair or walker, go to City Beach. To borrow a chair and have accessible restrooms close by, Bolsa Chica is the most complete. Huntington State Beach has the most ramps.
  3. Go early in the afternoon. Around Thanksgiving the sun sets at about 4:45 p.m. (see November and December weather). The state beach visitor centers close at 4, and the gates close at 9 p.m.
  4. Check for rain. After a real rain, stay out of the ocean for 72 hours. The rainy-day guide explains why and has indoor ideas.
  5. For the evening, Visit Huntington Beach says the concrete paths at Huntington State Beach lead to the bonfire pits. The bonfire guide covers rings and rules.

Away from the sand, the Bolsa Chica wetlands are another good outing. The south-lot footbridge is ADA compliant, and the boardwalks allow a lot of wheelchair access.

Wheelchair beach access FAQ

Can you borrow a beach wheelchair in Huntington Beach?

Yes, for free. Beach wheelchairs are lent at Huntington City Beach ((714) 536-8083, or Zack's by the Pier at (714) 536-0215), at the Magnolia kiosk at Huntington State Beach ((714) 536-1454) and at Bolsa Chica State Beach ((714) 377-5691). They are manual chairs a companion pushes. City Beach may have only one, so call a day ahead.

Where are the Mobi-Mats in Huntington Beach?

There are two on Huntington City Beach: one in front of the Waterfront Beach Resort by the Huntington Street entrance, and one north of the Pier. Visit Huntington Beach says they reach the water's edge at high tide.

Is beach parking free with a disabled placard in Huntington Beach?

In the City's hourly lots, including Pier Plaza, Blufftop, Dog Beach, First and Atlanta, First and Walnut, and Warner Avenue, yes, in marked disabled spaces. The attended lot south of the Pier charges placard holders $15 a day from November through February, the same as everyone. The state beaches charge $7.50 only with a State Parks Disabled Discount Pass; a DMV placard alone does not qualify.

Can visitors from other states get the California Disabled Discount Pass?

State Parks says out-of-state placards are accepted as proof. The pass costs $3.50 and is good for five years. Processing time is not published, so apply well before your trip.
