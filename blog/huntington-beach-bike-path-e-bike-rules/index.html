Biking the Huntington Beach Bike Path: E-Bike Rules, Speed Limits and Where You Can't Ride

By Mats, SuperhostUpdated October 30, 2026

Short answer: Yes, you can ride a regular bike or a Class 1 or Class 2 e-bike on the paved Huntington Beach beach path. The City limit is 10 mph, and 5 mph whenever people are walking nearby. You cannot ride on the sand, and you must walk your bike on the Pier and Pier Plaza. Everyone under 18 needs a helmet. Nobody under 16 may ride a Class 3 (28 mph) e-bike, and police can impound one if they do. Since 2023, City police can write a $150 ticket for e-bike violations.

This guide is for the family that has just walked up to a rental stand near the Pier, with a teenager asking for the fast e-bike and nobody sure what the rules are. It comes from the City's beach rules in Huntington Beach Municipal Code chapter 13.08, the Huntington Beach Police Department's e-bike safety and rules guide, the California Highway Patrol's May 2026 e-bike bulletin and OC Parks' e-bike rules, all checked October 30, 2026.

The route: Bolsa Chica to the Santa Ana River

The paved path runs about 8.4 miles one way (TrailLink), between the sand and Pacific Coast Highway. From north to south:

  • Bolsa Chica State Beach. The northern end, run by California State Parks, with fire rings and parking right behind the sand. The Bolsa Chica Ecological Reserve across PCH is a different place, and bikes are not allowed there at all.
  • Dog Beach and the bluffs. The path passes the off-leash beach north of Goldenwest Street.
  • City Beach and the Pier. The busiest stretch. Expect people crossing to the sand, strollers and kids on scooters. This is where the 5 mph rule matters most, and where the Pier and Pier Plaza are walk-only.
  • Huntington State Beach. Wide and quieter, run by State Parks again, down to the Santa Ana River at the Newport Beach line.

At the river you can turn inland on the Santa Ana River Trail, the path in the photo above. That trail is run by Orange County, not the City, and its rules are a little different: Class 1 and Class 2 e-bikes only, 10 mph, and every bike yields to pedestrians (OC Parks). A relaxed round trip from the Pier to the river and back is about 7 miles.

Which e-bike can you ride, and where?

California sorts e-bikes into three classes (CHP Bulletin 285). Rental stands should be able to tell you which class each bike is; the class is also on a label on the frame.

State law changed recently, so older articles are out of date. The CHP's May 2026 bulletin says e-bikes of every class can now go anywhere a bike can, unless a local agency bans them on a trail or State Parks bans them on its paths. We could not find a City rule that bans Class 3 bikes from the beach path. In practice, a Class 3 makes little sense here. You cannot use its speed on a path capped at 10 mph, you cannot take it onto the Santa Ana River Trail, and nobody under 16 can ride it.

A bike that goes faster than 20 mph on the motor alone, or has no working pedals, is not an e-bike. The CHP says those cannot be ridden on public roads or paths, and police can tow them. That includes a bike that has been "unlocked" to go faster.

If you have kids or teens with you

  • Helmets for everyone under 18, on regular bikes and e-bikes (CVC 21212, listed in the HBPD guide). Ask the rental stand if helmets are included; if not, bring your own.
  • No Class 3 for anyone under 16 (CVC 21213). Police can impound a Class 3 bike ridden by someone too young, and the owner may have to show proof of a safety course to get it back.
  • No riding double unless the bike has a real passenger seat. The HBPD guide lists improper passengers as a violation.
  • No earbuds in both ears and no phone in hand while riding (CVC 27400 and 23123.5, in the HBPD guide).
  • Agree on a meeting point, such as a lifeguard tower number, before a teenager rides ahead.

Riding after dark in November

Clocks go back on Sunday, November 1, 2026, and sunset drops to about 5 p.m., then 4:45 p.m. by Thanksgiving (see our November weather and sunset guide). An afternoon ride can easily end in the dark. After dark a bike needs a white front light, a red rear reflector and side and pedal reflectors (CVC 21201). E-bikes need a red rear reflector or red rear light at all hours. Rental bikes often have no lights, so ask. The City beach closes at 10 p.m.

Tickets and impounds

In September 2023 the City Council gave police the power to write a $150 civil citation for a first e-bike offense, or a criminal infraction that ABC7 reported can cost about $450 with court fees. Officers can also take e-bikes away from underage riders and return them to parents. Patrol officers were assigned to watch for e-bike violations near schools, businesses and the beach. Rules visitors most often miss: the 5 mph limit near people, the sidewalk ban downtown (no riding in business districts or next to schools, churches and playgrounds, HBMC 10.84.160), and walk-only Pier Plaza.

Renting a bike: what to ask

Bike and e-bike rental stands sit along the path near the Pier and in the beach lots. We are not listing shops or prices here because they change. Before you pay, ask:

  1. What class is this e-bike, and does it have a throttle?
  2. Are helmets and a lock included?
  3. Does it have lights, if we might be out near sunset?
  4. Can we take it onto the Santa Ana River Trail? (Only Class 1 and Class 2 can go there.)
  5. What happens if it gets a flat far from the stand?

What could change

In September 2025 the City Council asked the police chief to come back with stronger e-bike rules, including where e-bikes can ride near schools and churches (Voice of OC). As of October 30, 2026 we found no new ordinance in the City code, but one could follow. State Parks can also ban e-bikes on the paths at Bolsa Chica and Huntington State Beach; we found no such ban, so check the signs at each entrance. Posted signs and the current City code win over this guide.

Huntington Beach bike path FAQ

Are e-bikes allowed on the Huntington Beach bike path?

Yes, on the paved path only. The City Council lifted its 2017 e-bike ban on the beach path in April 2021. E-bikes are still not allowed on the sand, and no bike may be ridden on the Pier or Pier Plaza. On the State Beach stretches at Bolsa Chica and Huntington State Beach, posted State Parks signs apply.

What is the speed limit on the Huntington Beach beach path?

10 mph unless a higher speed is posted, and 5 mph whenever pedestrians are present, under Huntington Beach Municipal Code 13.08.280. Where yellow lights are flashing on the beach service road, slow to a walking pace of 2.5 mph between the marked points. On the Pier and Pier Plaza, bikes must be walked.

Can a 14-year-old ride a rented e-bike in Huntington Beach?

A Class 1 or Class 2 e-bike, yes, with a helmet, because California requires helmets for every bike rider under 18. A Class 3 e-bike, no. State law bars anyone under 16 from riding a Class 3 e-bike, and police can impound one ridden by an underage rider.

How long is the Huntington Beach bike path?

About 8.4 miles one way, from Bolsa Chica State Beach in the north to the Santa Ana River at the Newport Beach line, according to TrailLink. At the river you can turn inland on the Santa Ana River Trail, where Orange County allows Class 1 and Class 2 e-bikes at 10 mph.

What happens if you get caught speeding on an e-bike in Huntington Beach?

Since a 2023 city ordinance, Huntington Beach police can write a $150 civil citation for a first offense or a criminal infraction, which ABC7 reported can cost about $450 with court fees. Officers can also impound e-bikes in some cases, such as a Class 3 bike ridden by someone under 16.
