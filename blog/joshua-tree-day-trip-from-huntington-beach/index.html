Joshua Tree Day Trip from Huntington Beach: A Winter Plan That Fits the Daylight

By Mats, SuperhostUpdated November 13, 2026

Short answer: yes, Joshua Tree works as a day trip from Huntington Beach, but it is a long one. The West Entrance is about 130 miles from north Huntington Beach, roughly 2½ to 3 hours each way with no traffic, so plan on 5 to 6 hours in the car. Winter is the best season to go (days around 60°F), but the sun sets at about 4:35 to 4:45 p.m. in December, and there is no food, gas or drinking water inside the park. Leave the coast by 6 a.m., enter at the West Entrance, see Hidden Valley, Barker Dam, Keys View and Skull Rock, and be driving out by sunset. Entry is $30 per car for 7 days, and the entrance stations take cards only.

This guide is for families and friends staying on the coast over Thanksgiving, Christmas or New Year's who want to see a national park while they are in Southern California, and who are trying to decide whether one long day is worth it. Park facts come from the National Park Service (fees, trails, visitor centers, weather), checked November 13, 2026. Distances and no-traffic drive times are OpenStreetMap routing estimates from north Huntington Beach (92649). Sunset times are calculated for Hidden Valley with the NOAA solar formulas and are approximate; the mountains to the west can put you in shade a little earlier.

Is it worth it for one day?

For most groups who have never been, yes, if you treat it as a full day out and keep the plan short. The famous part of the park, the Joshua trees and giant boulder piles along Park Boulevard, is a 25-minute drive in from the West Entrance, and most of the classic stops are short, flat walks next to parking lots. You do not need to be a hiker to have a good day.

It is not worth it if your group gets carsick, if you have small children who won't do 5 or more hours in car seats, or if you only have one free day and the beach is the reason you came. In those cases, two easier half-day ideas from the house are the Bolsa Chica wetlands and the winter tide pools. If what you really want is mountains and maybe snow, the Palm Springs Aerial Tramway is about 106 miles from the house and you can do it in an afternoon; the snow day trip guide covers it. If you want to see the park properly, with sunrise, sunset and the stars, stay one night in Joshua Tree, Twentynine Palms or Palm Springs instead of driving back.

A one-day route that fits the daylight

  1. 6:00 a.m. Leave Huntington Beach. Fill the tank or charge the car before you go, and fill up again in Yucca Valley or Joshua Tree village on Highway 62. Bring lunch, snacks and more water than you think you need: the NPS says there is no water in the interior of the park.
  2. About 8:45 a.m. Joshua Tree Visitor Center (6554 Park Boulevard, Joshua Tree; open 7:30 a.m. to 5 p.m.). Last flush toilets and café before the park, a paper map, and rangers who know which roads and trails are open that day. The West Entrance is 5 miles south.
  3. Hidden Valley (about 25 minutes from the West Entrance). A 1-mile loop with about 100 feet of climbing, roughly an hour, inside a ring of boulders that the NPS says was once rumored to hide cattle rustlers. This is the best single stop if you only do one walk. Joshua trees line the road all the way in.
  4. Barker Dam, a short drive from Hidden Valley. A 1.1-mile loop, about an hour, to a small dam and water tank built by early cattle ranchers. The NPS says to watch for bighorn sheep here.
  5. Keys View around midday. A 20-minute drive down Keys View Road from Park Boulevard to an overlook on the crest of the Little San Bernardino Mountains. You look down into the Coachella Valley, across to San Jacinto Peak behind Palm Springs and the usually snow-capped San Gorgonio Mountain, and on a clear day you can see the Salton Sea and the San Andreas Fault line in the valley below. The lookout is wheelchair accessible; a 0.2-mile loop climbs the ridge for a slightly better view.
  6. Cap Rock and Skull Rock in the afternoon. Cap Rock is a 0.4-mile loop at the Keys View Road junction. Skull Rock is right beside Park Boulevard near Jumbo Rocks Campground, so you can see it from the parking area; the full Skull Rock Nature Trail is a 1.7-mile loop if you have time.
  7. By about 4:30 p.m. head out. Turning back to the West Entrance is the shorter way home. In late afternoon the low sun on the rocks is the best light of the day, so stop for photos on the way out, but don't start a trail you can't finish before dark. Nights drop below freezing.

A Thursday variation: on the way home, Palm Springs is about an hour from the West Entrance. From October through May, VillageFest, the city's street fair, closes Palm Canyon Drive downtown every Thursday from 6 to 10 p.m., with food and late shop hours (VillageFest). Dinner there breaks up the drive, and Palm Springs to the house is about 2 hours more. Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve and New Year's Eve all fall on Thursdays in 2026, so check the VillageFest calendar before you count on those nights.

Fees and passes

  • $30 per private vehicle, a 7-day pass for everyone in one non-commercial car. $25 for a motorcycle, $15 per person on foot or bike, free for 15 and under (NPS fees page, updated September 29, 2026).
  • Annual passes: the Joshua Tree annual pass is $55 for U.S. citizens and residents. The America the Beautiful pass, good at all national parks for a year, is $80 for U.S. residents and $250 for non-U.S. residents. If you are visiting from abroad and may see a second national park within a year, compare those prices before you pay at the gate.
  • How to pay: the West and North Entrance stations take debit or credit cards only. The visitor centers take cash or card. You can also buy a digital America the Beautiful pass on Recreation.gov ahead of time and show it on your phone.

What winter is actually like in the park

The NPS describes winter days as around 60°F with freezing nights, and says it occasionally snows at the higher elevations. Coming from the coast, the change catches people out: it can be pleasant at noon in a T-shirt and well below freezing an hour after sunset. Pack a warm jacket, hat and gloves for everyone, closed-toe shoes for the cactus and rocks, sunscreen and sunglasses (the air is dry and clear), and a full change of socks.

The NPS also says holidays are among the busiest times of the year, and the West Entrance line can be long on a holiday weekend. Arriving before about 9 a.m. is the simplest way to avoid both the line and full parking at Hidden Valley and Barker Dam. Two more things from the park: don't trust your phone's GPS for routes inside or around the park, because it sometimes sends cars onto sandy backcountry roads, and expect little or no cell signal once you are past the entrance. Download offline maps before you leave the house and pick up a paper map.

Pets: dogs may be no more than 100 feet from a road, picnic area or campground, on a leash of 6 feet or less, and are not allowed on any trail. With a dog along, most of the walks in this plan are off the table.

What we can't tell you

  • Holiday hours. The park itself never closes, but we could not find posted Thanksgiving or Christmas Day hours for the visitor centers. If you go on a holiday, call the park at (760) 367-5500 first.
  • Road and trail closures on your day. Trails and roads are closed for maintenance from time to time (the Cholla Cactus Garden trail, for example, was closed for maintenance from December 2024 to March 2025). Check the NPS alerts and conditions page the night before.
  • Traffic. Our drive times assume clear roads. On a holiday weekend the trip can take an hour or more longer each way.

Joshua Tree day trip FAQ

Can you do Joshua Tree as a day trip from Huntington Beach?

Yes, but it is a long day. The West Entrance is about 130 miles from north Huntington Beach, roughly 2½ to 3 hours each way with no traffic. Leave by 6 a.m., keep to the short stops along Park Boulevard, and drive out by sunset, which is about 4:35 to 4:45 p.m. in December.

How much does it cost to enter Joshua Tree National Park?

$30 per private vehicle for a 7-day pass, $25 per motorcycle, or $15 per person on foot or bike; ages 15 and under are free. The West and North Entrance stations take debit or credit cards only. The America the Beautiful annual pass is $80 for U.S. residents and $250 for non-U.S. residents.

What is Joshua Tree like in December?

The National Park Service says winter days are around 60°F and nights are below freezing, with occasional snow at higher elevations. Holidays are among the park's busiest times, so arrive before about 9 a.m.

What should I see in Joshua Tree in one day?

From the West Entrance: the 1-mile Hidden Valley loop, the 1.1-mile Barker Dam loop, the Keys View overlook (a 20-minute drive off Park Boulevard), and Cap Rock and Skull Rock beside the main road. All are short and easy.

Is there food, gas or water inside Joshua Tree?

No. The park has no gas stations, restaurants, stores or hotels, and no drinking water in its interior. Fill up and buy food in Yucca Valley, Joshua Tree village or Twentynine Palms, and bring all the water you need.
