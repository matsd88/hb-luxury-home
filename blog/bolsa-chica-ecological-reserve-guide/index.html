Bolsa Chica Ecological Reserve: Trails, Birding & Visitor Guide (2026)

By Mats, SuperhostUpdated October 23, 2026

Short answer: Bolsa Chica Ecological Reserve is a free, flat wetlands walk in Huntington Beach, about 10 minutes from north Huntington Beach (92649). Park at the north lot, 3842 Warner Avenue, beside the interpretive center, or the south lot, 18000 Pacific Coast Highway, across from Bolsa Chica State Beach. Fish and Wildlife lists the reserve open daily 6 a.m. to 8 p.m. The Conservancy says trails run sunrise to sunset. Check the sign at the lot. Dogs, bikes, and drones are not allowed on the reserve. For birds, come in the morning.

Where to park

Fish and Wildlife describes more than 1,300 acres of estuary, mudflat, marsh, dunes, and riparian habitat. The Conservancy calls it about 1,400 acres and one of the largest remaining saltwater marshes in California. Weekend lots fill quickly.

North lot, 3842 Warner Avenue, 92649. The center is here. Northbound on Pacific Coast Highway, turn right on Warner, then right into the lot. Southbound, turn left on Warner, then right. When it is full, the Conservancy points to a public lot across the street, near the Huntington Beach Fire Station.

South lot, 18000 Pacific Coast Highway, 92648. It faces the state beach entrance, halfway between Warner and Seapoint. Driving south from Seal Beach, you cannot turn in. Use the U-turn and come back northbound.

How far the trails are

Fish and Wildlife describes a 4-mile network. The Conservancy says about five miles. The Bolsa Chica Land Trust map lists these walks:

WalkDistance
Inner Bolsa Bay Loop1.59 miles
Wintersburg Channel berm loop1.2 miles
Brightwater Trail0.92 miles one way, 1.84 out and back
Mesa Trail0.74 miles one way, 1.48 out and back
Pocket Wetland Loop0.72 miles

Not counting Brightwater, that map totals 5.11 miles from the north lot and 5.03 from the south. Most paths are flat decomposed granite. The south-lot footbridge is ADA compliant, and the center, its restrooms, and the boardwalks allow considerable wheelchair access. Some stretches are uneven for a stroller.

When the birding is best

Fish and Wildlife says more than 200 bird species have been identified here, including osprey, black skimmer, terns, white-faced ibis, American avocet, and black-necked stilt. Amigos says the National Audubon Society has designated Bolsa Chica an Important Birding Area.

  • November through February: shorebirds, ducks, and loons. The Conservancy recommends the Mesa Trail at low tide.
  • April through August: nesting terns, avocets, black-necked stilts, and western snowy plovers. Start at the south lot, toward the nesting islands and dunes.
  • September, October, and March: quieter, with migrants and the occasional rare bird.

Morning is the best hour. Try the south-lot loop or the Pocket Loop near the Mesa Overlook. On a falling tide, arrive about an hour after the daytime high so shorebirds are on the mudflats.

The interpretive center and free tours

The north-lot center is about 1,400 square feet, with aquariums (a swell shark, sea stars, kelp bass, striped shore crabs, and a rockfish), live reptiles, and taxidermy birds. As of October 23, 2026, hours are 9 a.m. to 1 p.m. Monday and 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. Tuesday through Sunday. Call (714) 846-1114 if Monday matters, and recheck bolsachica.org. Restrooms are beside the building.

Tours are free, and parking fills, so confirm the date. The Conservancy meets at the center on the fourth Saturday, 10 a.m. to noon. Amigos meets at the south lot: a wetland tour the first Saturday from 9 a.m., groups every 15 minutes until 10:30, and a bird walk the last Saturday at 8:30 a.m. The 3-to-5-mile hike is the second Saturday of March, June, September, and December, at 8 a.m. at the Brightwater trailhead (Bolsa Chica Street and Brightwater Drive, street parking). The Land Trust leads tours. Both lots post a bulletin board.

Rules worth knowing

No dogs on the reserve. A dog may stay inside a vehicle in the lots. Service dogs are allowed. Bikes and drones are prohibited. The Land Trust map marks some public trails, separate from reserve trails, where a leashed dog is allowed. If the sign does not say that, leave the dog in the car.

Stay on the path and out of fenced nesting areas. Some oil areas may be closed. Kayaks, boats, and paddleboards need a Fish and Wildlife permit. Fishing is only beside the Warner walk bridge. From age 16, the Conservancy requires a permit. Southern Pacific rattlesnakes live here.

What to bring

Bring water, binoculars, a hat, sunscreen, closed-toe shoes, and a light layer.

Pair it with Bolsa Chica State Beach

The beach is across Pacific Coast Highway from the south lot. State Parks lists 17851 Pacific Coast Highway. Hours are 6 a.m. to 10 p.m., and the gates close at 9 p.m. Parking is separate: $15 a regular vehicle, or $20 from Memorial Day weekend through Labor Day weekend. Seniors 62 and older pay $1 less. The lot is cashless: scan the LAZ QR code. LAZ may add a service fee.

The beach visitor center (9 a.m. to 4 p.m. daily) is a different building. A leashed dog may use the paved 8.5-mile trail and the lots, not the sand. Close to 200 fire rings are first come, first served. See the bonfire guide and the surf guide. Then move the car across the highway.

A morning from the house

Bolsa Chica is about 10 minutes from the house. Check-in is 4:00 p.m. and checkout is 11:00 a.m., so go on a middle day. In winter, use the north lot and the Mesa Trail near low tide. Those months overlap gray whale season. From April through August, start early at the south lot for nesting birds.

Bolsa Chica FAQ

Are dogs allowed at Bolsa Chica Ecological Reserve?

No. Not on the reserve, except inside a car in the lots or as a service dog. Leashed dogs are marked only on some public trails, not reserve trails.

Is parking free, and what are the hours?

Reserve lots are free. Fish and Wildlife lists 6 a.m. to 8 p.m. The Conservancy says sunrise to sunset. Follow the posted sign if those differ.

How long are the trails?

About five miles. On the Land Trust map, loops run from 0.72 miles to 1.59 miles.

When is the best time to see birds?

Morning. November through February for shorebirds, ducks, and loons. April through August for nesting terns, avocets, stilts, and western snowy plovers.

When is the interpretive center open?

As of October 23, 2026: 9 a.m. to 1 p.m. Monday, and 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. Tuesday through Sunday, at the north lot. Call (714) 846-1114 to confirm.

Can I visit the beach the same day?

Yes. Bolsa Chica State Beach is across Pacific Coast Highway, open 6 a.m. to 10 p.m., with gates closing at 9 p.m. Parking there is paid and separate.
