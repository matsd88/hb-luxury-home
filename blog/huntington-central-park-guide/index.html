Huntington Central Park Guide: Shipley Nature Center, Disc Golf, Dog Park & More

By Mats, SuperhostUpdated October 28, 2026

Short answer: Huntington Central Park is the city's big green backyard: about 343 acres split into a West side and an East side along Goldenwest Street, with two lakes, the free Shipley Nature Center, an 18-hole disc golf course, a fenced dog park, the Equestrian Center, the Central Library and two lakeside-style cafes. Most of it is flat and stroller-friendly, and it's roughly a 10-minute drive from our home. It's the perfect half-day when you want a break from the sand.

Most visitors come to Huntington Beach for the ocean and never realize that one of the largest city-owned parks in Orange County sits right in the middle of town. Locals jog, bike, fish, walk dogs and picnic here every day. Here's how we tell our guests to spend a morning or afternoon in Central Park, side by side.

How the park is laid out

Central Park is divided by Goldenwest Street. Central Park West is the wilder side, with Lake Huntington, Shipley Nature Center, the disc golf course, the dog park and the Equestrian Center. Central Park East has Talbert Lake, the Central Library and theater, the Secret Garden and Butterfly Garden, the amphitheater and the HB Sports Complex. Paved paths with exercise stations loop through both halves, plus dirt trails through the trees, so you can walk or bike between the two sides. The city's main address for the park is 18002 Goldenwest Street.

Central Park West

Shipley Nature Center

If you only do one thing, make it Shipley. This roughly 10-acre native habitat preserve has more than 4,000 feet of wood-chipped trails through eight habitats, including oak woodland, meadow, a redwood grove and Blackbird Pond. The Shirley Dettloff Interpretive Center has hands-on exhibits, and its Kids Corner gets new self-guided activities every third Friday of the month.

  • Hours: Tuesday to Saturday, 9 AM to 1 PM, with later hours on the first Friday of each month
  • Admission: free, with a suggested $5 donation to the Friends of Shipley Nature Center
  • Parking: main lot at 17851 Goldenwest Street, just north of Goldenwest and Talbert
  • Heads up: it can close during heavy rain or strong winds, so check its website or social media first

Walk quietly and you'll usually spot birds, butterflies and turtles. It's a calm, shady contrast to a busy beach day.

Lake Huntington and the cafe

Lake Huntington is the classic Central Park scene: ducks, geese, herons, a loop path and plenty of benches. Grab breakfast or lunch at Kathy May's cafe by the lake, then let the kids burn off energy at the nearby playground. The city lists five playgrounds across the park, including one on the West side with a kid-sized zip line.

Huntington Beach Disc Golf Course

Established in 1977, this championship-caliber 18-hole, par 3 course winds through the wooded part of Central Park West. It's fun for beginners and serious players alike and is listed at 18189 Goldenwest Street. Pack a few discs if you have them. It's a great low-key group activity: everyone can play, and nobody needs a tee time.

Best Friend Dog Park

Traveling with a pup? The fenced, off-leash dog park off Edwards Street has separate areas for small and large dogs, with shade trees, benches and water stations. Small-dog hours are 9 AM to 7 PM on weekdays and 10 AM to 7 PM on weekends. The big-dog area closes earlier, at 6 PM on weekdays and 5 PM on weekends, after which all dogs can share the small area. Parking is in the lot on Edwards. Please check with your host before bringing a pet to any rental, including ours.

Huntington Central Park Equestrian Center

At the southwest end of the park, the Equestrian Center is home to around 400 horses, with arenas, annual horse shows and more than 150 acres of trails. Even if you're not riding, it's fun for kids to see the horses, and the center's website lists its current lessons and events.

Central Park East

Central Library and fountains

The Huntington Beach Central Library at 7111 Talbert Avenue isn't your average library. The striking building has big indoor and outdoor fountains, public art, a large children's section, a cafe run by the Friends of the Library and the adjacent Library Theater. It's a great rainy-day or hot-afternoon stop. Typical hours are Monday 1 to 9 PM, Tuesday to Thursday 9 AM to 9 PM, Friday and Saturday 9 AM to 5 PM and Sunday 1 to 5 PM, but confirm on hbpl.org before you go.

Talbert Lake, the Secret Garden and the Butterfly Garden

The East side is quieter and perfect for a slow walk. Circle Talbert Lake, then look for the Secret Garden and the Butterfly Garden tucked along the paths. Bring a picnic blanket, since there's lots of open grass and shade. For a bite, the Park Bench Cafe is known as one of the most dog-friendly restaurants in Huntington Beach.

HB Sports Complex

The Sports Complex at 18100 Goldenwest Street hosts youth soccer, softball and baseball tournaments most weekends. If your family is in town for a tournament, Central Park is your home field, and the beach is close enough for a post-game sunset.

A note on Adventure Playground

You may read about Adventure Playground, the beloved summertime mud-and-water play area. The city says it's closed for the 2026 season and until further notice, so don't plan your day around it.

A simple half-day plan

  1. 9 AM: Walk the trails at Shipley Nature Center while it's cool and the birds are active.
  2. 10:30 AM: Play nine holes of disc golf, or let the kids loose on the zip-line playground.
  3. Noon: Lunch by Lake Huntington at Kathy May's.
  4. 1:30 PM: Cross Goldenwest to the Central Library fountains and the Butterfly Garden.
  5. 4 PM: Head back to the house to rinse off, then drive about 10 minutes to the coast for sunset.

Tips before you go

  • The park has several parking lots, including ones off Goldenwest Street, Edwards Street and Slater Avenue, so park close to the side you're visiting.
  • Dogs must stay leashed outside the dog park, and a park guard patrols daily.
  • Bring water and sunscreen. Some trails are shady, but the open lawns get warm in the afternoon.
  • Rinse off sandy shoes first. The paved paths are great for bikes, scooters and strollers.
