Toll Roads and 405 Express Lanes Near Huntington Beach: A Rental-Car Guide

By Mats, SuperhostUpdated November 15, 2026

Short answer: you can drive from any of the three airports to Huntington Beach, and to the beach, Disneyland and San Diego, without paying a single toll. Two things catch visitors in rental cars. First, the 405 Express Lanes: the two left lanes in each direction on the I-405 between the 73 in Costa Mesa and the 605 at the L.A. County line, which runs right past Huntington Beach. They need a FasTrak transponder, and there is no way to pay afterwards by license plate unless you have an account. Stay to the right of the double white lines and the 405 is free. Second, the 73, 133, 241 and 261 Toll Roads, which phone maps like to use as shortcuts. They have no booths and take no cash, but you can pay online by license plate within 5 days of driving. Miss that window and the violation is $57.50 plus the toll. Before you leave the rental counter, ask how the car handles tolls and what it charges per day.

This guide is for families and friends picking up a rental car at John Wayne (SNA), Long Beach (LGB) or LAX for Thanksgiving, Christmas or New Year's in Huntington Beach, and for anyone whose phone just routed them onto a "toll road" and who wants to know whether a bill is coming. Toll rules and penalties come from the agencies that run the roads: the 405 Express Lanes (run by OCTA), The Toll Roads (73, 133, 241 and 261) and the 91 Express Lanes. We checked them on November 15, 2026.

None of these roads has a toll booth, and none takes cash. The Toll Roads stopped accepting cash in May 2014; everything is read electronically from a transponder or a photo of the license plate.

The 405 Express Lanes: stay right of the double white lines

From all three airports, the drive to Huntington Beach is on the I-405, and the 405 Express Lanes run along the whole Huntington Beach stretch. The lanes have their own entrances, marked by broken lines in the double white stripe. Signs at each entrance show the toll for the trip, and the price is locked in when you enter (405 Express Lanes FAQ). Besides the two ends at the 73 and the 605, you can enter or exit at Bolsa/Goldenwest, Magnolia/Warner and the 22 (entry and exit points). Two of those are Huntington Beach street names, so an express-lane sign can look a lot like your exit sign. Your exits are on the right.

  • Carpools still need a transponder. Cars with 3 or more people ride free at all times, and 2-person carpools are free outside peak hours for the lanes' first three and a half years, but only with a FasTrak Flex switchable transponder set to the right number of people. A rental car with a full load of family and no transponder pays a violation like anyone else.
  • What a violation costs: $25 per trip plus the toll. If you ignore the first notice, a delinquent notice adds $30, and an unpaid violation can go to collections with a hold on the vehicle's registration, up to $100 per violation (violation FAQ). In a rental car, the notice goes to the rental company, which passes it on to you with its own fee.
  • If you already have FasTrak at home (any California toll agency), you can add the rental's plate to your account as a temporary vehicle with start and end dates. Rental and temporary vehicles are the only ones that don't need a transponder on the 405 Express Lanes. Update the end date if you return the car early or late; the agency warns that wrong dates can lead to violations (rental vehicles page).

Coming from LAX or Long Beach: in Los Angeles County the 405's left lane is a regular carpool lane. Where the 405 meets the 605, just past Long Beach, the left lanes turn into the Express Lanes. If you were in the carpool lane, move right before that point. From John Wayne: the airport sits next to the southern end of the Express Lanes. Head north on the 405 in the regular lanes and stay there.

The Toll Roads: how to pay within 5 days

The 73, 133, 241 and 261 are Orange County's Toll Roads. Visitors usually end up on one of two: the 73, which runs from the 405 by John Wayne Airport south to the I-5 near Mission Viejo, and the 241, which phone maps use to skip traffic on the 91 heading inland. The first few miles of the 73 next to the 405 are free; heading south, it becomes a toll road just past the Jamboree Road and MacArthur Boulevard ramps in Newport Beach (route details).

If you drive one without a transponder, you have three options (The Toll Roads, rental vehicles):

  1. Pay it yourself online. Use the official Pay Rental Tolls page and enter the plate and your travel dates. You can pay up to 5 days before or after you drive, at the non-account rate. Some rental companies require you to register ahead of time, and trips from before you registered can still be billed through the rental company.
  2. Let the rental company handle it. You pay the tolls plus the convenience fee in your rental agreement. The Toll Roads says some Avis and Budget locations in California have cars with transponders that work on every California toll road and express lane for a fee.
  3. Add the plate to your own FasTrak account, if you have one, with start and end dates.

If nobody pays within those 5 days, each trip becomes a violation: $57.50 plus the toll, then another $42.50 if the first notice is ignored, and possibly a DMV registration hold after that (penalty FAQ). For a rental car, that means a bill from the rental company weeks after you're home. Toll prices change with the time of day and distance; the toll calculator shows the price for your trip.

Day trips where your phone may pick a toll road

  • Laguna Beach: Pacific Coast Highway is free, and it is the nicer drive anyway. The faster inland route, the 405 south to the 133 (Laguna Canyon Road), is also free. The tolled option is the 73. See the Laguna Beach day trip guide.
  • San Diego: the 405 to the I-5 is free the whole way. Phone maps sometimes suggest the 73 to skip the slow merge where the 405 joins the I-5 in Irvine; that saves a few minutes and costs a toll. Or skip the drive entirely: see the San Diego day trip guide for the train from Irvine.
  • Disneyland: no toll roads on the way.
  • Big Bear, Palm Springs and Joshua Tree: you will likely take the 91 east toward Riverside. Keep out of the 91 Express Lanes on the left, which need a transponder. Your phone may also suggest the 241 Toll Road to get around 91 traffic; if you take it, pay within 5 days. See the snow day trip guide and the Joshua Tree guide.

Three questions to ask at the rental counter

  1. Does this car have a toll transponder or a plate-based toll program, and is it on? If the car is already in a program, paying The Toll Roads yourself could mean paying twice.
  2. What does it cost? Ask whether the daily fee applies only on days you use a toll road or every day of the rental, and whether there is a maximum per rental. Fees differ by company and location and change often, so read your own agreement instead of trusting a number online, including any from us.
  3. Can I decline it and pay tolls myself? If you plan to avoid toll roads entirely, which is easy from Huntington Beach, you may not need the program at all.

Set your phone to avoid tolls, then still watch the lanes

Before the trip, turn on toll avoidance. In Google Maps, open your route, tap the three-dot menu, then Options, and turn on Avoid tolls. In Apple Maps, turn on Avoid Tolls in the iPhone Settings app (Apps, then Maps, then Driving), or in the route options before you start. That keeps you off the 73, 241 and other Toll Roads. It won't keep you out of the 405 Express Lanes, because they are part of the same freeway as the free lanes. Which lane you drive in is up to you, so stay right of the double white lines.

Ignore texts saying you owe a toll. The Toll Roads warns about a nationwide text scam asking drivers to pay unpaid tolls through a link. Pay only on the agencies' own websites or apps, or by phone: The Toll Roads at (949) 727-4800, the 405 Express Lanes at (562) 740-4405, the 91 Express Lanes at (800) 600-9191. In a rental car, a real bill comes from the rental company.

What we can't tell you

  • Your rental company's fee. These vary by company and location, so we don't quote them. The Toll Roads' rental page links to each major company's toll policy.
  • Holiday toll prices on the 405 Express Lanes. The lanes have separate holiday schedules and new rates as of November 1, 2026, but you can only see them by picking an entry, an exit and a holiday on the site. The toll is always posted at the entrance.
  • Whether a penalty can be waived. Neither agency publishes a first-time waiver policy for out-of-town drivers. If a notice arrives, call the agency before you pay the penalty.

Toll roads and rental cars FAQ

Are there toll roads between the airport and Huntington Beach?

No toll is needed. From John Wayne (SNA), Long Beach (LGB) or LAX, the regular lanes of the I-405 take you to every Huntington Beach exit for free. Just stay out of the 405 Express Lanes, the two left lanes behind the double white lines between the 73 in Costa Mesa and the 605.

Can I use the 405 Express Lanes without FasTrak?

No. A FasTrak transponder is required, even for carpools. The only exception is a rental or temporary vehicle whose plate has been added to a FasTrak account. Otherwise it is a violation: $25 plus the toll, then $30 more if the first notice is ignored.

I drove on the 73 Toll Road in a rental car. How do I pay?

Pay online at The Toll Roads' official Pay Rental Tolls page (thetollroads.com) within 5 days of driving, using the plate number and your travel dates. Check first whether your rental company's toll program already covers the car. After 5 days, unpaid trips become violations of $57.50 plus the toll each.

Do Orange County toll roads take cash?

No. The 73, 133, 241 and 261 have had no cash booths since May 2014. Tolls are charged to a FasTrak transponder, a plate-based account, an online payment within 5 days, or through your rental company.

Does Avoid Tolls in Google Maps or Apple Maps keep me out of the express lanes?

It keeps you off toll roads like the 73 and 241, but not out of the 405 or 91 Express Lanes, which run next to free lanes on the same freeway. Lane choice is up to you: stay to the right of the double white lines.
