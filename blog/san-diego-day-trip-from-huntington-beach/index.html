San Diego Day Trip From Huntington Beach: Train or Drive, and What Fits in One Day

By Mats, SuperhostUpdated November 3, 2026

Short answer: yes, San Diego works as a day trip from Huntington Beach, but pick one part of the city, not three. The easiest way is the Amtrak Pacific Surfliner from Irvine Station (free parking, about 30 minutes from the house): the daily 8:12 a.m. train reaches Old Town San Diego at 9:52 a.m. and downtown's Santa Fe Depot at 10:07 a.m., much of it right along the ocean, and the 7:01 p.m. train from Santa Fe Depot gets you back to Irvine at 8:52 p.m. Driving is about 95 miles each way, just under 2 hours with no traffic and a lot longer on a Friday afternoon. Drive if you want La Jolla, where the train doesn't go. Take the train if you want Old Town, the downtown waterfront or December Nights in Balboa Park (free, Friday December 4 and Saturday December 5, 2026).

This guide is for groups staying in Huntington Beach over Thanksgiving, December or New Year's who have one free day and keep asking "can we do San Diego?" It answers how to get there, which train, what fits in a day, and what to skip. Train times are from Amtrak's Pacific Surfliner timetable as printed for November 3, 2026, and service notices from Pacific Surfliner alerts, both checked November 3. Distances and no-traffic drive times are from OpenStreetMap routing from north Huntington Beach (92649).

Taking the Pacific Surfliner

The nearest stations to Huntington Beach are Santa Ana (1000 E. Santa Ana Blvd., 16.7 mi, about 25 minutes from the house) and Irvine (15215 Barranca Pkwy., 20.6 mi, about 30 minutes). The same trains stop at both, Santa Ana 11 minutes earlier. Irvine is the one I'd use: Pacific Surfliner's station page lists free parking in a 1,500-space structure, limited to 72 hours. South of Irvine, the train runs along the beach at San Clemente and by San Onofre and Del Mar. Heading south, sit on the right-hand side for the ocean.

  • Buy tickets in the Amtrak app or online. The ticket windows at Santa Ana and Irvine are closed, according to the alerts page. Fares change with the date and how full the train is, so check the price for your group before you decide; for five or more adults, the car can come out cheaper.
  • Check the alerts page the night before and the morning of. The track along the bluffs south of San Clemente is exposed to surf and landslides. This September, high surf damaged it over Labor Day weekend and trains between Irvine and Oceanside were replaced by buses for about a week. On November 3, the alerts page said trains through San Clemente may run about 30 minutes late.
  • No trains on November 7 and 8, 2026. Pacific Surfliner lists no train service between San Diego and San Luis Obispo that weekend for track work, and no replacement buses. Pick another day or drive.
  • Old Town or Santa Fe Depot? Get off at Old Town for Old Town San Diego State Historic Park, which is next to the station. Stay on to Santa Fe Depot for downtown, the waterfront and Little Italy, all within walking distance.

Driving to San Diego

The drive is I-405 south to I-5 south, about 95 miles to downtown. OpenStreetMap routing puts it at just under 2 hours with no traffic. In real traffic, the stretches through Irvine and Mission Viejo and the drive into San Diego can add an hour, especially on weekday afternoons and the Wednesday before Thanksgiving. Leave by 7:30 a.m. and head home after 7 p.m., or the reverse commute will catch you both ways.

Near the house, some of the 405 carpool lanes are now the tolled 405 Express Lanes, which need a FasTrak transponder. If you're in a rental car, read the rental vehicle page or stay in the regular lanes.

Drive when your day is La Jolla (about 87 miles, just under 2 hours), when you want two stops the train doesn't link, or when you have car seats and a lot of gear.

What fits in one day (pick one)

1. Old Town and the downtown waterfront (train)

Take the 8:12 a.m. from Irvine and get off at Old Town at 9:52. Spend the morning in the state historic park and lunch in Old Town, then ride a later Pacific Surfliner or the MTS Green Line trolley down to Santa Fe Depot. From there the harbor, the USS Midway Museum, Seaport Village and Little Italy are all within about a mile on foot. Catch the 5:01 or 7:01 p.m. back. It's the easiest day for grandparents and kids, and nobody has to drive home.

2. La Jolla Cove and the seals (drive)

Leave early: parking near the Cove is mostly on the street and hard to find later in the day. Walk the coast path from La Jolla Cove to the Children's Pool to see the sea lions and harbor seals. From December 15 to May 15 each year, the Children's Pool beach is closed for harbor seal pupping season, but you can still watch from the sidewalk and, when the surf allows, the seawall (10News on the closure). Keep your distance from the animals. Head home after 7 p.m., or before 3 p.m.

3. December Nights in Balboa Park (train, December 4 or 5)

December Nights is San Diego's big free holiday festival, with lights, food, music and the museums of Balboa Park. In 2026 it runs Friday, December 4, 4 to 10 p.m. and Saturday, December 5, noon to 10 p.m. The city says it draws more than 350,000 people over the two days, so parking near the park is very limited and the drive home afterward is slow. The train makes it easy: arrive at Santa Fe Depot, take a rideshare or the city's event transit to the park (see the event's Getting There page for 2026 details), and be back at the depot for the 8:01 or 9:01 p.m. to Irvine. On the Saturday, you can go at noon and catch the 7:01 p.m.

On other days, if you drive to Balboa Park, note that the city charges non-residents for parking in the park through the end of 2026: $10 for up to 4 hours or $16 a day in the premium lots, $10 a day in the general lots, and the first 3 hours free at Lower Inspiration Point, enforced 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. (City of San Diego). The city's page says paid parking ends January 1, 2027.

Plan the day from Huntington Beach

  1. Choose train or car by where you're going, not by habit. Old Town, downtown or December Nights: train. La Jolla or beaches: car.
  2. Check the date. No trains on November 7 and 8. December Nights is only December 4 and 5. The Children's Pool beach closes December 15.
  3. Look at the Pacific Surfliner alerts the night before and again in the morning.
  4. Leave the house by 7:30 a.m. for the 8:12 from Irvine. You'll want time to park and walk to the platform.
  5. Pack layers. December days near the water are mild, but evenings drop into the 50s and below; see the November and December weather guide.

San Diego day trip FAQ

How far is San Diego from Huntington Beach?

About 95 miles from north Huntington Beach to downtown San Diego by I-405 and I-5, just under 2 hours with no traffic. Weekday afternoons and holiday weekends can add an hour or more.

Can you take the train from Huntington Beach to San Diego?

Not from Huntington Beach itself. The closest Amtrak Pacific Surfliner stations are Santa Ana (about 25 minutes from north Huntington Beach) and Irvine (about 30 minutes, with free parking). From Irvine, the train takes about 1 hour 40 minutes to Old Town San Diego and about 1 hour 55 minutes to Santa Fe Depot downtown.

What time is the first train to San Diego from Irvine?

On the timetable printed for November 3, 2026, the first daily southbound train leaves Irvine at 7:12 a.m. and reaches Santa Fe Depot at 9:04 a.m., followed by 8:12 and 9:12 a.m. trains. Times can change, so check Amtrak for your date.

When is December Nights 2026 in Balboa Park?

Friday, December 4, 2026, from 4 to 10 p.m., and Saturday, December 5, 2026, from noon to 10 p.m. It is free. Parking near Balboa Park is very limited, so the train to Santa Fe Depot plus a short ride to the park is the easier way from Orange County.

Is the train between Orange County and San Diego running?

On November 3, 2026, yes, with possible delays of about 30 minutes through San Clemente. There is no train service on November 7 and 8, 2026, and no replacement buses. The coastal track near San Clemente has closed several times after storms, so check pacificsurfliner.com/alerts before you go.
