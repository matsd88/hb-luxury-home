Newport Beach Christmas Boat Parade 2026: Where to Watch Free, Passing Times and Parking

By Mats, SuperhostUpdated November 18, 2026

Short answer: The 118th Newport Beach Christmas Boat Parade runs Wednesday, December 16 through Sunday, December 20, 2026, from about 6:30 to 9:00 p.m. each night. Watching from shore is free. The boats take two and a half hours to loop the harbor, so the time they pass depends on where you stand: about 6:54 p.m. at Marina Park, 7:15 at the Balboa Fun Zone, around 8:00 on Balboa Island and about 8:45 at Lido Marina Village. For most families driving down from Huntington Beach, the easiest plan is Balboa Village: park in the 614-space Balboa Pier lot ($3.10 an hour in winter), watch the 6:45 drone show, and see the boats at 7:15. Be parked by 5:30.

This guide is for visitors staying in Huntington Beach the week before Christmas who want one night at the Newport parade and need to know where to stand, when the boats get there and where the car goes. Dates, times and the route are from the organizers' 2026 parade site and its official 2026 route map. Parking rates are from the City of Newport Beach parking page. We checked everything on November 18, 2026.

The route and the 6:30 start are the same every night. The grand marshals change, and opening night adds fireworks and a market.

Drone shows run every night from two spots. Over the Balboa Bay Resort: 6:00 and 8:15 p.m. on Wednesday, 6:15 and 8:15 p.m. Thursday to Sunday. Over Peninsula Park, next to the Fun Zone and Balboa Pier: 6:45 and 8:00 p.m. every night. The organizers say the fireworks go ahead only if it is safe to fire them.

The parade site's FAQ says up to 100 boats take part, from kayaks to yachts over 100 feet, and the Marina Park page calls the loop a 14-mile route. You do not need all five nights. One is plenty.

Five free places to watch, and who each one suits

1. Balboa Village and the Fun Zone (boats at 7:15)

This is our pick for a first visit. The biggest parking lot on the route is two blocks away, the official map marks restrooms by the pier, there are restaurants and snack stands along the boardwalk, and the 6:45 drone show goes up next door half an hour before the boats arrive. The organizers call the Fun Zone a great spot for first-timers. The trade-off is that it is far down the Peninsula, on a road with one way in and one way out, so getting home takes patience.

2. Marina Park (boats at about 6:54)

Best with small children. The 10.5-acre park has a nautical-themed playground, a picnic area, room to run around and a 177-space lot, and the boats pass early, so you can be back in Huntington Beach by about 8:30. The official map marks public restrooms a block east, by Veterans Memorial Park. It is the first public stop on the route and the largest. On opening night expect the biggest crowd here, because of the market and the opening ceremony. In 2025 the Marina Park lot was closed to the public on opening night. The 2026 page doesn't say either way, so on Wednesday plan to park somewhere else.

3. Balboa Island's bayfront walk (boats from 7:54 to 8:11)

A public walkway circles the whole island, and the parade goes all the way around it, so you can stroll past the decorated waterfront houses while you wait. That is the most festive setting on the route. It is also the hardest place to park. The City's parking-lot list has no lot on Balboa Island, only residential streets. The easier way is to park at the Balboa Pier lot and cross as a walk-on passenger on the Balboa Island Ferry from the Fun Zone. The ferry company lists hours of 6:30 a.m. to midnight every day. Bring a few dollars in cash for the fare. We could not confirm how the ferry runs while the parade crosses its path, so call 949-673-1070 before you rely on it.

4. Corona del Mar: Lookout Point, Pirate's Cove and China Cove (boats at about 7:32)

The boats swing around a turning marker just inside the harbor entrance, and Lookout Point on Ocean Boulevard looks down on that turn from the bluff. The map marks restrooms there. You avoid the Peninsula traffic completely because you come in from Coast Highway. Parking is on neighborhood streets or in the Corona del Mar Main Beach lot at Jasmine and Ocean (545 spaces, open 6 a.m. to 10 p.m.; from October to April it is $3.50 an hour on weekdays and $5.90 an hour on weekend evenings). The view is from farther away than at the Fun Zone, so this suits adults and older kids more than toddlers who want the boats close.

5. Peninsula Point street ends (boats at about 7:25)

Past the Fun Zone, the map marks small public beaches at the bay ends of the streets toward the jetty, and West Jetty View Park at the tip. They are farther from the lots and the restaurants. Expect a long walk from the Balboa Pier lot or a hunt for street parking.

Want a late dinner instead? Lido Marina Village and Lido Park see the boats last, at 8:46 to 8:50 p.m. You can eat first and walk out to the water at the end.

If you would rather pay for a seat

  • Newport Sea Base, 1931 W. Coast Highway. Reserved water's-edge seating. Seating opens at 7:00 p.m. and the parade is in view from about 8:37 p.m. Parking there is limited, at $25 a vehicle. The viewing page does not list a seat price, so check the booking link on the official viewing page.
  • Waterfront restaurants. The map marks "Dine and Watch" restaurants along the Peninsula, Mariner's Mile and Lido. The organizers say to book early. Ask what time the boats pass that table. A Mariner's Mile table doesn't see them until after 8:40.
  • On a boat. Charter and harbor cruise boats sell parade-night tickets. The organizers warn that most sell out early.

Getting there from Huntington Beach

Newport Harbor is the next big harbor south. From our house in the 92649 zip code it is about 13 miles to Marina Park and about 15 to Balboa Village, and from the Huntington Beach Pier it is about 6 miles to Marina Park. On an ordinary evening that is under half an hour. On parade nights it is not, because every car bound for the Peninsula funnels onto Newport Boulevard and then Balboa Boulevard.

  • Route. Take Pacific Coast Highway south and follow the Newport Boulevard / Balboa Peninsula exit. That matches the organizers' own directions. None of it is a toll road.
  • Leave early. The sun sets at about 4:45 p.m. that week. Leave Huntington Beach by 4:30 and aim to be parked by 5:30. That leaves time to eat and find a spot at the rail.
  • For Corona del Mar, stay on Coast Highway past Newport Boulevard and across the bay bridge, then turn right toward Ocean Boulevard. You skip the Peninsula entirely.
  • Rideshare saves the parking search but sits in the same line of cars. Set the pickup for the ride home a few blocks back from the water.
  • Going home. Thousands of people leave by the same road. Either walk to the car as soon as the last boats have passed your spot, or stay for the second drone show and dessert and leave after 9.

A plan that works: Balboa Village on a weeknight

  1. 4:30 p.m. Leave Huntington Beach.
  2. 5:15 to 5:30. Park in the Balboa Pier lot. Walk the pier while it is still dusk.
  3. 5:45. Dinner or snacks around Main Street and the Fun Zone. Sit-down places fill up on parade nights, so book ahead or keep it casual.
  4. 6:30. Pick your spot on the boardwalk rail.
  5. 6:45. Drone show over Peninsula Park.
  6. 7:15. The first boats arrive. The line takes a while to pass.
  7. 8:00. Second drone show, then walk back to the car.

Thursday is our choice of night. It has the full parade and the drone shows without the opening-night crowd or the weekend one. That is a judgment call, not a statistic. The organizers do not publish attendance by night.

Rain, wind and cancellations

The parade runs rain or shine. The one thing that cancels it is a small craft advisory from the Coast Guard. If one is issued, that night's parade is called off, and the organizers say the decision is made at 6:00 p.m. That is after you would have left Huntington Beach, so check the forecast before you go and look at the parade's Instagram or call 949-729-4400 if the wind is up. The parade site does not mention make-up nights. If the parade matters to your trip, don't save it for Sunday.

December evenings on the water are cold by local standards. The average December low at the coast is about 50°F, and there is usually a breeze on the bay. Bring a real jacket for everyone, a blanket for the kids and something to sit on if you are at a beach or park. Our November and December weather guide has the numbers.

Newport's parade or Huntington Harbour's Cruise of Lights?

They are different things, and you can do both. In Newport you stand on shore and decorated boats sail past you. In Huntington Harbour's Cruise of Lights (December 19 to 23, 2026) you buy a ticket, ride a boat and look at decorated waterfront homes. The Cruise of Lights is in our own zip code. Newport is the bigger spectacle and it is free.

The two overlap on Saturday and Sunday, December 19 and 20. A 5:30 p.m. cruise in Huntington Harbour will not get you to Newport for the boats. Do Newport on Wednesday, Thursday or Friday and the cruise on the weekend or after.

For lights without the parade crowds, use a different night. The floating Lighting of the Bay at Newport Dunes and the snowfall tree at Fashion Island are free, and our Christmas lights guide has the 2026 dates and times.

Boat parade FAQ

When is the Newport Beach Christmas Boat Parade in 2026?

The 118th parade runs Wednesday, December 16 through Sunday, December 20, 2026. Boats leave the tip of Lido Isle at about 6:30 p.m. and finish there at about 9:00 p.m. each night.

Is the Newport Beach boat parade free?

Yes. Watching from shore is free, and the organizers say you can see it from almost anywhere on the harbor. You pay only for parking, a restaurant table, reserved seating at the Newport Sea Base or a ticket on a charter boat.

What time does the boat parade pass Marina Park and the Balboa Fun Zone?

The official 2026 map lists Marina Park at 6:54 p.m. and the Balboa Fun Zone at 7:15 p.m. The parade's web pages say about 6:45 for Marina Park, so be at the water by 6:40. Balboa Island's bayfront is 7:54 to 8:11 p.m. and Lido Marina Village about 8:46 p.m.

Where is the best place to watch the boat parade with kids?

Marina Park at 1600 W. Balboa Blvd. It is free, it has a playground, and the boats pass early, at about 6:54 p.m. For older kids, Balboa Village adds a drone show at 6:45 p.m. and has the biggest parking lot on the route.

Where do you park for the Newport Beach boat parade?

The Balboa Pier lot at Palm Street and Balboa Boulevard has 614 spaces at $3.10 an hour from October to April. Marina Park has 177 spaces. The City's lot list has no lot on Balboa Island. Lots fill early, so aim to be parked by 5:30 p.m.

Is the boat parade cancelled if it rains?

No. It runs rain or shine. It is cancelled for the night only if the Coast Guard issues a small craft advisory, and the organizers say that decision is made at 6:00 p.m.

Are there fireworks at the Newport Beach boat parade in 2026?

On opening night only: Wednesday, December 16, at about 6:15 p.m. off Newport Pier, if conditions are safe. Drone shows run every night, over Peninsula Park at 6:45 and 8:00 p.m. and over the Balboa Bay Resort at 6:15 and 8:15 p.m. (6:00 and 8:15 on opening night).

How far is the boat parade from Huntington Beach?

Marina Park is about 6 miles down Pacific Coast Highway from the Huntington Beach Pier and about 13 miles from our house in 92649. It is under half an hour on a normal evening and longer on parade nights, so leave by 4:30 p.m.
