Family Photos in Huntington Beach: Do You Need a Permit, Where to Go, and When

By Mats, SuperhostUpdated November 7, 2026

Short answer: You don't need a permit for family photos in Huntington Beach if the pictures are for your own use. The City says so plainly for the Pier, City Beach and its parks, and that holds whether you use a phone on a tripod or hire a photographer for a family session. A permit only comes in when the photos are commercial: ads, products, stock. The two state beaches, Bolsa Chica and Huntington State Beach, are permitted by the state instead, and their rule is worded around money: anyone doing photography for profit needs a state permit. If you're paying a photographer and want no doubt at all, shoot on City Beach. For light, start about an hour before sunset. Sunset is around 4:45 p.m. over Thanksgiving week and 4:50 p.m. at Christmas, and on both holidays the tide is very low at about that hour, which gives you the shiny wet sand in the photo above.

This guide is for a family that only gets everyone together once a year, has booked Thanksgiving or Christmas week by the beach, and wants this year's holiday card photo taken on the sand. I read the City's film office pages, its 2026 permit fee sheet and the California Film Commission's rules on November 7, 2026. Tide times are NOAA predictions. Sunset times are for the Pier, worked out with NOAA's solar equations, and may differ from your weather app by a minute.

Do you need a permit? It depends on the beach

Huntington Beach has two owners along the sand, and they don't use the same rules.

If you're hiring a photographer

On City property you're covered either way. The City's FAQ asks "Do engagement or family photo sessions need a permit?" and answers no, as long as the photos are for personal use. The state's wording is different. It talks about the person doing the photography for profit, not about what you do with the pictures. A paid portrait session at Bolsa Chica fits that wording, so ask your photographer whether they pull state permits, or just pick a spot on City Beach. A few more things to ask before you book:

  • Which beach, exactly? "Huntington Beach" might mean the Pier (City) or Bolsa Chica (state). It matters for the permit, for parking and for crowds.
  • Drone shots? For permitted shoots, the City requires drone insurance and an FAA-licensed pilot who stays under 400 feet and in line of sight. If a drone is part of the package, ask how they handle that.
  • Anyone going in the water? On a permitted shoot, the City requires a Marine Safety Officer for anything in the water. For a family shoot, keep it to wet sand and ankle-deep at most. The water is around 60–64°F in December anyway.
  • The rain plan. December averages about three rainy days at the coast. Ask how a rain date works before you pay a deposit.

When to shoot: light and tide

The best family light is the last hour before sunset. The sun is low, faces aren't squinting, and the sky turns orange behind the Pier. Then wait 10 to 20 minutes after sunset: the sky goes pink and blue, and the Pier's lamps come on. That's when the photo above was taken.

Tide matters more than most people expect. At high tide, waves come right up the beach and the background is all whitewater. At a low tide there's a wide strip of wet, flat sand that reflects the sky and the Pier. This year the holidays line up well. Those minus tides are also good for tide pooling, so a clear holiday afternoon could be tide pools at Little Corona, then photos back in Huntington Beach at sunset. Huntington Beach itself is all sand, so there are no rocks in the photos here.

Five spots, and which one suits your family

1. Beside the Pier (City Beach)

This is the classic Huntington Beach picture: the Pier and its lifeguard tower behind you, Catalina on the horizon on a clear day. Stand on the sand a little way off either side of the Pier so its pilings run into the frame. It's also the busiest spot in town, especially on a sunny holiday afternoon, so you'll have strangers in the background. Arrive early, and try a lower angle so the people further away end up behind your family.

Park: the Main Promenade structure at 200 Main Street is free for 90 minutes, which covers a quick session. The Pier Plaza lot right beside it is $3 an hour. Details are in the parking guide.

2. Dog Beach and the bluffs (City Beach)

North of the Pier, between Goldenwest and Seapoint, the beach sits below a bluff. You get the same sunset with far fewer people, and from the top of the bluff you can fit the whole family and a long sweep of coast in one frame. Dog Beach is still City Beach, so there's no permit question. It's also the one stretch of City Beach where dogs are allowed on the sand, so if the family dog is in the photo, this is the spot. Expect a curious visitor or two in the frame. The Blufftop lots are $3 an hour.

3. Bolsa Chica State Beach

Wide, flat and quiet, and the closest beach to our house. It's the easiest place to get a background with nobody in it. If you're taking your own photos, it's a great choice. If you're paying a photographer, read the state permit section above first. Parking is $15 a day and cashless. Sunset-and-bonfire is a lovely combination here, and the bonfire guide covers the fire rings and the gate times.

4. Huntington Central Park (City park)

For a portrait with trees, grass and a lake, and no wind or sand, try Central Park. It's a City park, so family photos need no permit, and on a gray or windy day it's often the better choice. Light under the trees fades early, so start 90 minutes before sunset. The Central Park guide has the parking lots.

5. Back at the house

For a rainy day, the youngest kids or just tired legs, there's no shame in the backyard. At our house there's a big lawn and a pergola with a BBQ island. Set a phone on a tripod, use the self-timer, and the pool table makes a good second backdrop indoors.

Doing it yourself with a phone

  • Bring a small tripod. Push the legs into the sand so the wind doesn't knock it over. A shot with everyone in it needs the self-timer or a phone-to-watch remote.
  • Put the sun behind the group, then off to one side. Straight into the sun gives silhouettes. Turn everyone a quarter turn so the light hits one side of the faces.
  • Take a lot of photos. With little kids, you'll get one where everybody looks at the camera by burst number 30. Use burst mode or just keep tapping.
  • Watch the waves. At low tide a bigger set can still run far up the flat sand. Keep the tripod and the bags above the last wet line.
  • Dress for 55°F. Coastal lows are about 55°F in November and 50°F in December, and the sand cools fast after sunset. Bring jackets for between shots, and a towel for sandy feet.
  • Don't block the path. The beach bike path behind the sand is busy at sunset. Set up on the sand, not on the path. The bike path guide has the rules.

Hours: City Beach is closed 10 p.m. to 5 a.m. The state beaches are open 6 a.m. to 10 p.m., and their gates close at 9 p.m. Sunset photos don't come close to either limit.

What I couldn't confirm

  • State Parks' review fee for a portrait photographer at Bolsa Chica. The Film Commission doesn't charge, but it doesn't list State Parks' fees on the pages I read. Its office is at 323-860-2960.
  • Whether every photographer bothers with the state permit. That's between them and the state. The point for you is that City Beach takes the question away.
  • The weather on your day. Check the National Weather Service forecast for Huntington Beach the day before, and keep a second evening free if the photo matters.

Family photos in Huntington Beach FAQ

Do I need a permit to take family photos in Huntington Beach?

No, not if the photos are for your own use. The City of Huntington Beach says still photos for personal use, such as engagement or family photos, don't need a permit on the Pier, City Beach or its parks. At Bolsa Chica and Huntington State Beach, personal photos taken without commercial equipment don't need a state permit either.

Does a hired photographer need a permit at the Huntington Beach Pier?

Not for a family session whose photos are for your personal use. The City's FAQ says family photo sessions don't need a permit in that case. A commercial shoot does: a $150 application fee, $150 a day and a 15% coordination fee, with insurance and at least 5 business days' notice. At the two state beaches, the state requires a permit for anyone doing photography for profit, so ask your photographer about it there.

What time is sunset in Huntington Beach at Thanksgiving and Christmas 2026?

About 4:45 p.m. over Thanksgiving week and about 4:50 p.m. on Christmas Eve and Christmas Day. Start family photos about an hour earlier. NOAA predicts low tides near sunset on both holidays: 4:49 p.m. on Thanksgiving and 4:37 p.m. on Christmas Day.

Where is the least crowded beach for family photos in Huntington Beach?

Bolsa Chica State Beach and the beach below the bluffs near Dog Beach are much quieter than the Pier. The Pier is the most recognizable background but also the busiest, especially on a sunny holiday afternoon.
