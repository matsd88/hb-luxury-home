Snow Near Huntington Beach: A Day Trip to Big Bear, Mountain High or the Palm Springs Tram (Winter 2026–27)

By Mats, SuperhostUpdated November 1, 2026

Short answer: Huntington Beach never gets snow, but real snow is about two hours' drive away in a normal winter. The three easiest options from north Huntington Beach are Snow Valley in Running Springs (about 90 miles, the closest Big Bear Mountain Resort mountain), Mountain High in Wrightwood (about 88 miles), and the Palm Springs Aerial Tramway (about 107 miles), where a cable car lifts you to 8,516 feet and you never drive on a snowy road. None of the resorts has an opening day yet for 2026–27: both Big Bear and Mountain High name November 21, 2026 as the earliest possible date, weather permitting. Before you drive up, carry chains, check Caltrans QuickMap, and book tubing and parking a day ahead.

This guide is for families and groups staying on the coast over Thanksgiving, Christmas or New Year's whose kids have never touched snow, and who want to know where to go, whether they need chains, and what it costs before they give up a beach day for it. Distances and no-traffic drive times are from OpenStreetMap routing from north Huntington Beach (92649). Resort details come from Big Bear Mountain Resort, Mountain High and the Palm Springs Aerial Tramway; chain rules come from Caltrans. All were checked November 1, 2026.

Big Bear: Snow Valley, Snow Summit and Bear Mountain

Big Bear Mountain Resort runs three mountains: Snow Valley in Running Springs, and Snow Summit and Bear Mountain in Big Bear Lake. For a family day trip from Huntington Beach, Snow Valley is the one to pick. The resort describes its Coyote Creek tube park as the closest to the Los Angeles area, and it is about 90 miles from the house versus about 103 miles to Snow Summit. Snow Summit has a second tube park, Grizzly Ridge.

  • Opening day is not set. The resort's hours page says November 21, 2026 is the earliest the mountains may open and that it probably won't know until about mid-November, when it is cold enough to make snow. Its Thanksgiving weekend page says opening dates for skiing, snowboarding and tubing "have not yet been determined." On November 1 the tubing page listed Coyote Creek as "closed until December."
  • Tubing and snow play: $20 to $64 per person depending on the day, and a ticket is required for every guest, infants and seniors included. Buy at least a day ahead; same-day tickets are only sold on site if any are left. A tubing session is 1 hour 30 minutes of unlimited laps. Riders must be at least 42 inches tall and 40 pounds. Kids' waterproof snow bibs rent for $20, helmets for $10.
  • Parking is a separate ticket. On busy dates, Snow Valley requires a parking ticket ($25–$30), and the resort says plainly that there are no remote lots, no shuttle and no public parking in Running Springs. A tubing reservation does not include parking. Paid-parking dates this season include December 4–6 and December 11–31, 2026, January 1–10, January 15–18 (MLK weekend) and February 12–21 (Presidents' Day). Snow Summit and Bear Mountain upper lots are $30–$40 on those dates, with free remote lots and a free shuttle (parking page).

Mountain High in Wrightwood

Mountain High is in the San Gabriel Mountains above Wrightwood, about 88 miles from the house. It calls itself "Southern California's closest winter resort with no mountain driving," because you approach from the desert side via I-15 and Highway 138 rather than up a long grade. There is still a stretch of Highway 2 into the mountains, and chain controls can apply there too.

  • Opening: the resort homepage lists opening day as November 21 and says it is weather dependent. Its Yeti's Snow Play page lists November 27, 2026.
  • Yeti's Snow Play: tubing lanes, sledding and a snow play area. Children must be at least 36 inches tall to tube or sled alone.
  • Parking: $30 every day at Yeti's Snow Play. Arrive early.

Palm Springs Aerial Tramway: snow without the snowy road

The tram starts at the Valley Station in Palm Springs, at 2,643 feet, and climbs to the Mountain Station at 8,516 feet in Mount San Jacinto State Park. The drive from Huntington Beach is about 107 miles east through Riverside and out I-10, and it stays at low elevation the whole way, so chains are not part of the plan. When there is snow at the top, kids can play in it in the state park, and the Winter Adventure Center rents snowshoes ($18 a day) and cross-country skis ($21 a day) to adults 18 and over, first come, first served.

  • Tickets (official page): adults $36.95, children 3–10 $20.95, seniors 65+ $33.95, each including a $2 online fee. Children under 2 ride free but need a boarding pass from the ticket booth on the day. Children under 16 must be with an adult 21 or older. If tickets sell out online, the booth still sells same-day tickets.
  • Hours, October 5, 2026 to May 27, 2027: first tram up at 10 a.m. on weekdays and 8 a.m. on weekends and holidays; last tram up at 8 p.m., last tram down at 9:30 p.m.
  • Parking: $15 per car (parking page). The lot opens at 9 a.m. on weekdays and 7 a.m. on weekends and holidays.
  • The catch: snow at the top is not guaranteed. The tram runs year-round, and early in the season there may be little or none. Check the tram's live camera and weather page before you go. No pets.

Chains: what California actually requires

When a mountain highway has chain controls, Caltrans posts signs with the level, and the California Highway Patrol can cite you if you don't follow them. These are the three levels, from the Caltrans chain controls page:

  • R-1: chains are required on all vehicles except passenger cars and light trucks under 6,000 pounds with snow tires on at least two drive wheels. Those cars must still carry chains.
  • R-2: chains or traction devices are required on all vehicles except four-wheel or all-wheel drive vehicles with snow-tread tires on all four wheels. Even those must carry traction devices.
  • R-3: chains on every vehicle, no exceptions. Caltrans says a highway is often closed before it reaches R-3.

A "snow-tread tire" is marked M+S, M/S or MUD AND SNOW on the sidewall. You usually get about a mile between the "Chains Required" sign and the checkpoint, and the speed limit with chains on is 25 or 30 mph. Chain installers on the mountain are independent businesses, not Caltrans, and they are not allowed to sell or rent chains, so buy yours in the flatlands before you go. If you are driving a rental car, ask the rental company whether chains are allowed on it before you buy any.

Before you leave the house, check Caltrans QuickMap or call the Caltrans highway information line at (800) 427-7623 for chain controls and closures on Highways 330, 18, 38, 2 and 138.

A snow day plan from Huntington Beach

  1. The day before: buy tubing or snow play tickets and, on a paid-parking date, the parking ticket. Check the resort's hours page and the weather. Put chains, gloves, and a full change of clothes for everyone in the car.
  2. Leave early: about 6:30 to 7 a.m. for Snow Valley or Mountain High, so you arrive before the lots fill. For the tram, leaving around 8 a.m. on a weekday puts you at the Valley Station near the 10 a.m. first car.
  3. Fill up or charge before you start the climb, and keep the tank well above empty: traffic stopped at a chain checkpoint still burns fuel and heat.
  4. Dress for wet, not just cold. Jeans and sneakers soak through fast in snow play. Waterproof pants or bibs, boots, gloves and a spare pair of socks make the difference between a three-hour day and a 40-minute one.
  5. Head down by mid-afternoon, before the roads refreeze at dusk. In December the sun sets before 5 p.m.

Snow near Huntington Beach FAQ

Where is the closest snow to Huntington Beach?

In a normal winter, the closest snow play areas are Snow Valley in Running Springs, about 90 miles from north Huntington Beach, and Mountain High in Wrightwood, about 88 miles. Each is roughly two hours' drive with no traffic. The Palm Springs Aerial Tramway, about 107 miles away, reaches 8,516 feet without any snowy driving, but snow at the top is not guaranteed.

When do Big Bear and Mountain High open for 2026–27?

Neither has a confirmed date. On November 1, 2026, Big Bear Mountain Resort said November 21, 2026 is the earliest its mountains may open and that it probably won't know until mid-November. Mountain High lists opening day as November 21, weather dependent, and its Yeti's Snow Play area lists November 27.

Do I need chains to drive to Big Bear?

Caltrans tells drivers to always carry chains in the mountains in winter. Under R-1 and R-2 chain controls, some cars with snow tires, or 4WD or AWD with snow-tread tires on all four wheels, may drive without chains on, but they must still carry them. Under R-3, every vehicle needs chains. Check Caltrans QuickMap or call (800) 427-7623 before you leave.

How much is snow tubing at Big Bear?

Big Bear Mountain Resort lists tubing and snow play at $20 to $64 per person, depending on the date, with a ticket required for every guest including infants. Tube riders must be at least 42 inches tall and 40 pounds. Parking at Snow Valley is a separate $25 to $30 ticket on busy dates, including December 11–31, 2026.

Can you see snow from the Palm Springs tram without driving in snow?

Yes, when there is snow on the mountain. The tram climbs from 2,643 feet in Palm Springs to 8,516 feet in Mount San Jacinto State Park, and the drive to the Valley Station stays in the desert. Tickets are $36.95 for adults and $20.95 for children 3–10, and parking is $15.
