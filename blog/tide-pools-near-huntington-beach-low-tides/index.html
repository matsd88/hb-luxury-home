Tide Pools Near Huntington Beach: The Best Low Tides for Thanksgiving, Christmas and Winter 2026–27

By Mats, SuperhostUpdated October 31, 2026

Short answer: Huntington Beach itself has no real tide pools. Its shoreline is sand. The closest rocky tide pools are at Little Corona del Mar Beach in Newport Beach, about 30 minutes south, and Crystal Cove State Park just past it. Go on an afternoon minus tide. The best ones this winter fall on Mon–Wed, November 23–25 (Thanksgiving week), Tue–Thu, December 22–24 (Christmas week), and January 19–23. On Thanksgiving Day and Christmas Day the lowest water comes right at sunset, so the day before is the better bet. Everything in both places is no-take: look, don't collect.

This guide is for the family staying in Huntington Beach over the holidays whose kids want to see crabs, anemones and sea stars, and who need to know which day and what time before they load the car. The tide times are NOAA predictions for the Newport Bay Entrance station (feet above mean lower low water, local time), and the sunset times are calculated for Corona del Mar. The rules come from the California Department of Fish and Wildlife, the City of Newport Beach and California State Parks, all checked October 31, 2026.

The best tide-pool afternoons, November 2026 to February 2027

A "minus tide" is a low tide below zero on the chart, and it uncovers reef that is usually underwater. In Southern California winter, the lowest tides of the year come in the afternoon, which is easy for visitors, but they slide later each day toward sunset. Early November also has a smaller set of afternoon lows, about −0.4 to −0.5 ft on November 8–10 around 2:45 to 4 p.m. They are worth a visit if that is when you are in town. Black Friday (November 27) and the weekend after Christmas are a bust in daylight: the lowest water comes after sunset.

How to use the table: arrive about an hour before the low tide time, explore as the water drops, and start back as soon as it turns. Little Corona and Crystal Cove are a few miles from the Newport Bay Entrance station, so treat the times as close, not exact. Predictions are not guarantees, either. A big swell can keep the reef wet even on a minus tide. Recheck the NOAA page and the surf forecast the day before.

Three of these windows overlap the California Coastal Commission's King Tides dates (November 24–26, December 23–25 and January 21–22). The same pull that makes the very high morning tides makes the very low afternoon ones, so a morning walk to see the high water and an afternoon tide-pool visit can happen on the same day.

Little Corona del Mar Beach (Newport Beach)

Little Corona is a small cove at the end of Poppy Avenue at Ocean Boulevard in Corona del Mar. It is the closer of the two, about 17 miles and roughly 30 minutes from north Huntington Beach with no traffic (OpenStreetMap routing), straight down Pacific Coast Highway. Holiday afternoons on PCH are slower.

  • The walk down: a long paved ramp from Ocean Boulevard, with no stairs. It is steep enough that a stroller is work on the way back up.
  • Parking: there is no lot. Park on the residential streets near Poppy and Ocean and read every sign. On a holiday low tide the spaces go early, so plan to arrive well before the low.
  • Restrooms: sources disagree about restrooms at Little Corona, so don't count on them. Use a restroom before you leave or at Corona del Mar State Beach next door.
  • Where the pools are: on the dark reef flats and rocks at the edge of the cove, like the ones in the photo above. Most of them are underwater until the tide drops below about zero.

Little Corona sits inside the Crystal Cove State Marine Conservation Area, whose boundary starts at Corona del Mar State Beach (CDFW). The City of Newport Beach puts the rule plainly: no removing or collecting, even temporarily, from tide pools in the protected areas, which include every beach from Corona del Mar State Beach south. That includes putting a crab in a bucket to show someone. You may see volunteer tidepool docents on busy low tides; the City's marine protection program trains them to explain the rules and point out animals.

Crystal Cove State Park

Crystal Cove is the big state park between Corona del Mar and Laguna Beach, with 3.2 miles of beach in seven coves (State Parks info sheet). Its address is 8471 North Coast Highway, about 20 miles and a little over 30 minutes from north Huntington Beach. Reef Point and Pelican Point are the tide-pool areas visitors usually name.

  • Hours: day-use areas are open 6 a.m. to sunset. That matters on Thanksgiving and Christmas, when the low tide comes at sunset.
  • Parking: $15 per car per day in the main lots, $20 on holidays and summer weekends. Los Trancos is $5 an hour.
  • No dogs on the beach (service animals excepted), and no collecting shells or rocks anywhere in the park.

Check closures first this year. High surf from Tropical Storm Rachel in October damaged the park. State Parks' October 15, 2026 update said the beach access trail at Pelican Point (P2) is closed after a bluff failure, the beach at Moro Cove is closed because of a cracked bluff, and parts of the Historic District beach are closed. On October 31 the park page still said "open with restrictions." Call the park at (949) 494-3539 or read the alerts before you drive. If Pelican Point is still closed, Little Corona is the simpler plan.

Tide-pool rules and manners

  • Take nothing. No animals, shells, rocks or plants, alive or "empty." Empty shells become homes for hermit crabs. This is state law in the conservation area, not a suggestion.
  • Touch gently, or not at all. Never pry an animal off a rock. A sea star or limpet that is pulled off can die.
  • Step on bare rock and sand, not on mussels, anemones or seaweed.
  • Don't flip rocks. If a child turns one over anyway, put it back exactly as it was.
  • Leave the bucket and net in the car.

Staying safe on a winter low tide

  • Never turn your back on the water. Winter swells send bigger sets in without warning, even on a minus tide. Keep small children on the inside of you, away from the edge of the reef.
  • Wear shoes that can get wet, such as old sneakers or water shoes. The rocks are slick with algae and sharp with barnacles. Flip-flops are the most common mistake.
  • Watch the time. Set a phone alarm for the low tide time and head back when it goes off. Water returns over the flats faster than people expect.
  • Stay off the bluffs and away from the base of the cliffs. State Parks warns that cliff faces are unstable, and this fall's storm made that worse.
  • Bring a layer. Late afternoon in December is cool and gets dark quickly. The November and December weather guide has the temperatures.

A simple tide-pool afternoon from Huntington Beach

  1. Pick the day from the table, and work back: leave the house about 1 hour 45 minutes before the low tide time.
  2. Eat first. Have lunch in Huntington Beach or along PCH on the way, so nobody is hungry when the water is lowest.
  3. Arrive about an hour before the low, find parking, and walk down.
  4. Explore for about an hour and a half, then head up as the tide turns and the light goes.
  5. Stay for sunset from the bluff on Ocean Boulevard, then drive home.

If you want more of a day trip, Laguna Beach's tide pools at Heisler Park, Shaw's Cove and Treasure Island have their own no-take rules, covered in our Laguna Beach day trip guide. For a nature outing without driving south, the Bolsa Chica wetlands are 10 minutes from the house and best for birds, not tide pools.

Tide pools near Huntington Beach FAQ

Are there tide pools in Huntington Beach?

Not really. Huntington Beach's shoreline is sand from Bolsa Chica to the Santa Ana River. The closest rocky tide pools are at Little Corona del Mar Beach in Newport Beach, about 17 miles and roughly 30 minutes from north Huntington Beach, and at Crystal Cove State Park just south of it.

When is the best low tide for tide pooling in November and December 2026?

By NOAA predictions for the Newport Bay Entrance, the best daylight minus tides are Monday November 23 (2:23 p.m., −1.1 ft) and Tuesday November 24 (3:09 p.m., −1.4 ft), then Tuesday December 22 (2:20 p.m., −1.5 ft) and Wednesday December 23 (3:05 p.m., −1.8 ft). On Thanksgiving and Christmas Day the low tide comes at about sunset.

Can you take shells or animals from Little Corona or Crystal Cove?

No. Both are in the Crystal Cove State Marine Conservation Area, where taking living marine resources from tide pools is prohibited. The City of Newport Beach also bans removing or collecting anything from tide pools, even temporarily, and State Parks prohibits collecting shells and rocks in Crystal Cove State Park.

How much is parking at Crystal Cove State Park?

The State Parks info sheet lists $15 per vehicle per day, $20 on holidays and summer weekends, and $5 an hour at the Los Trancos lot. Day-use areas are open from 6 a.m. to sunset. Little Corona has no lot, only street parking nearby.

Is Crystal Cove open after the October 2026 storm?

Mostly. On October 31, 2026 the State Parks page said the park was open with restrictions. Its October 15 update listed the Pelican Point (P2) beach access trail, the Moro Cove beach and parts of the Historic District beach as closed. Call (949) 494-3539 before you go.
